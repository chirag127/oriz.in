Investing starts with time horizon and risk capacity, not with a product name. A mutual fund, an index ETF, a bond and a fixed deposit solve different problems. The useful question is not “which gives the highest return?” but “which one fits the job this money has to do, and what happens if the market is down when I need it?”

This guide covers the core of the ORIZ.IN investing library: cost, asset allocation, funds, bonds, SIPs and portfolio review. It does not promise a return or choose a product for you.

## The order of operations

1. **Build the emergency reserve.** Investing comes after the money that prevents an emergency from forcing a sale.
2. **Clear high-cost debt.** A credit-card balance at a high rate is usually a better guaranteed “return” than a new investment.
3. **Set the goal and horizon.** Money needed in two years does not belong in equity. Money needed in twenty can tolerate more volatility.
4. **Choose the asset mix.** Equity, debt, gold and cash play different roles. The mix should match the goal and your ability to stay invested.
5. **Minimise cost and complexity.** Expense ratios, advisory fees and taxes compound too.
6. **Review, do not tinker.** A scheduled review catches drift; constant changes usually add cost and reduce discipline.

## Mutual funds and index funds

A mutual fund pools money and buys a portfolio according to its mandate. An index fund tracks a published index. The [direct versus regular guide](/money-finance/investing/mutual-fund-direct-vs-regular/) explains the commission embedded in regular plans. The [index fund comparison](/money-finance/investing/index-funds-india-nifty-50-vs-nifty-500/) explains the difference between large-cap concentration and a broader market.

The [factsheet guide](/money-finance/investing/how-to-read-a-factsheet/) shows which numbers matter: expense ratio, portfolio concentration, turnover, benchmark and manager tenure. The [SIP timing guide](/money-finance/investing/sip-timing-myth-and-math/) separates the useful part of systematic investing from the marketing.

A SIP is a cash-flow habit, not a guarantee. It buys more units when prices are low and fewer when they are high, but the final result still depends on the asset, the cost and the time horizon.

## Bonds, P2P and alternative fixed income

A bond is a loan to an issuer. Government securities, state development loans, treasury bills and corporate bonds carry different credit and interest-rate risks. The [retail bond guide](/money-finance/investing/bond-market-for-retail-rbi-retail-direct/) explains the RBI Retail Direct route and the risk ladder.

P2P lending and invoice discounting advertise higher yields because they carry credit, platform and liquidity risk. The [P2P guide](/money-finance/investing/p2p-lending-platforms-risks/) and the [invoice discounting guide](/money-finance/alternatives/invoice-discounting-platforms-returns/) price the default math. A high headline yield is not a substitute for underwriting.

## Portfolio review

A portfolio is not finished on the day it is bought. The [rebalancing guide](/money-finance/money-tools/portfolio-rebalancing-spreadsheet-method/) explains the band method; the [two-year audit](/money-finance/money-tools/two-year-audit-your-own-portfolio/) is a longer review. The [fear-greed gauge](/money-finance/money-tools/fear-greed-index-api-tickertape/) is a sentiment indicator, not a buy signal.

Use the [compound-interest calculator](/tools/compound-interest/), [CAGR calculator](/tools/cagr-calculator/) and [SIP calculator](/tools/sip-calculator/) to test assumptions. A projected corpus is an illustration, not a promise; change the return, inflation and contribution assumptions and see how fragile the answer is.

## What this guide will not do

It will not tell you that a particular fund, stock or bond is safe. It will not predict the market. It will help you compare costs, understand the role of each asset, and build a review habit that survives a bad year. Start with [direct versus regular](/money-finance/investing/mutual-fund-direct-vs-regular/) and [the factsheet guide](/money-finance/investing/how-to-read-a-factsheet/), then use the calculators with your own numbers.
