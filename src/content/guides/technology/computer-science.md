keyQuestions:
  - question: What does a diff algorithm do?
    answer: It finds a sequence of insertions, deletions or replacements that turns one text into another. The result depends on the algorithm and the cost model.
  - question: Why does the same change produce different diffs?
    answer: Different tools use different alignment rules, tokenisation and move detection. The output is an aid to review, not a mathematical proof of minimal change.

  - question: What is LCS?
    answer: The longest common subsequence is a sequence that appears in both inputs in order. It is a common basis for line diffs.
  - question: Are diffs always optimal?
    answer: No. Tools trade optimality for speed and readability, and they may not detect moved blocks.
