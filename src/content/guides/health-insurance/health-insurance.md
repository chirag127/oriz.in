Health insurance is judged at the hospital counter, not at the brochure. The premium matters, but the claim is decided by the room category, the deductible, the waiting period, the sub-limits and the policy wording. A ₹5 lakh cover with a restrictive room-rent clause can pay less than a ₹3 lakh policy with cleaner terms.

This guide covers the health-insurance decisions that affect an Indian household: how much cover to buy, how to read the clauses, how a super top-up works, and what to do before a claim.

## Start with the cover, then the price

Estimate the cost of a serious hospital episode in your city: room, ICU, surgeon, anaesthesia, diagnostics, medicines, implants and post-discharge care. Then compare that number with the sum insured and the sub-limits. A family floater may be efficient, but check how the sum insured is shared and whether a single claim can exhaust it.

Employer cover is a useful supplement. It is not a substitute for a personal policy that survives a job change. If you leave the employer, check the portability window and the waiting-period credit before buying a new policy.

## The clauses that decide the bill

- **Room-rent capping.** A percentage-of-sum-insured or category limit can trigger proportionate deductions across the claim. The [room-rent guide](/health-insurance/health-insurance/health-insurance-room-rent-capping/) works through the arithmetic.
- **Waiting periods.** Pre-existing diseases, specific conditions and maternity benefits may have different clocks. The [waiting-period guide](/health-insurance/health-insurance/waiting-periods-health-insurance/) explains the common 30-day, two-year and four-year periods.
- **Deductible.** The amount you pay before the policy responds. A deductible lowers the premium but raises the out-of-pocket bill.
- **Co-payment.** A percentage of every claim you pay. A large co-payment can turn a generous sum insured into a modest payout.
- **Sub-limits.** Caps on specific treatments, implants, diagnostics or room categories.
- **Network and cashless.** A cashless facility at a network hospital is useful, but the final bill can still include non-payable items.

## Super top-ups and riders

A super top-up sits above the base policy and a deductible. It can add a large layer of cover at a lower premium, but it is useful only if you can fund the deductible when the claim happens. The [super top-up guide](/health-insurance/health-insurance/super-topup-health-plans/) works through the example.

Riders can fill specific gaps, but they are not automatically worth buying. The [riders guide](/health-insurance/health-insurance/riders-worth-buying-and-worthless-ones/) separates useful protection from marketing.

## Before a claim

Call the insurer or third-party administrator before a planned admission and follow the pre-authorisation process. Keep the policy number, the network hospital confirmation, the estimated cost and the treating doctor's details. For an emergency, inform the insurer as soon as practical and keep every bill, prescription and discharge document.

If a claim is rejected, read the wording and ask for the specific clause. The [term-insurance claim guide](/health-insurance/life-insurance/term-insurance-claim-rejection-reasons/) covers the rejection patterns that also appear in health claims. Escalate through the insurer's grievance process and the policyholder channels; do not rely on a verbal assurance.

## What this guide will not do

It will not recommend a policy or guarantee a claim. It will help you compare sum insured, room-rent rules, deductibles, waiting periods and claim mechanics before you buy.
