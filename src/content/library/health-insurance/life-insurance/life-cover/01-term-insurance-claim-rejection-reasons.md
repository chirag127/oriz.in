Term insurance is bought on a promise that will be tested once, by people who are not you. The claim-rejection
rate in Indian life insurance is genuinely low — the industry settled roughly 98% of individual death claims by
count in recent IRDAI annual reports — but a low aggregate rate is cold comfort when the rejected claim is
yours and the family's income statement depends on it. Rejections are not random. They cluster around a short
list of causes, most of which are fixable years before the claim arises.

## The reasons, ranked by frequency

**1. Non-disclosure or misstatement of medical history.** The dominant cause by a distance. The proposal form's
health questions — hospitalisation in the last five years, diagnosed conditions, medication, smoking, alcohol —
are answered "no" out of optimism or impatience. The insurer's claim investigation then pulls the deceased's
hospital records under the family's consent and finds the diabetes diagnosis from 2019, or the two-year-old
hypertension prescription. Material non-disclosure — anything that would have changed the underwriting decision
or the premium — is grounds for repudiation.

**2. The contestability window and Section 45.** Under Section 45 of the Insurance Act, 1938, a life insurer
_cannot_ call a policy in question after **three years** from policy issuance — and since the 2015 amendment,
even within the first three years the insurer must prove fraud or material misstatement, and a policy can be
repudiated only by recording grounds in writing after an inquiry. The practical structure:

| Claim arises                   | What the insurer can do                                                                     |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Within 3 years of issuance     | Investigate; repudiate only for proved fraud or material misstatement, with written grounds |
| Within 3 years, policy revived | The 3-year window runs separately from revival date                                         |
| After 3 years                  | No repudiation for misstatement; claim payable as per terms                                 |

The three-year rule is the strongest protection a claimant has — and it is why the _first three years of
premiums should be paid with extra care about disclosure accuracy_, not less.

**3. Nomination and claimant-documentation failures.** The claim is not rejected; it stalls. A policy nominated
to a father who has died, with no successor nominee; a nominee under a will dispute; a claimant who is not the
nominee and must prove legal heirship through succession documentation. Under Section 39 of the Insurance Act
(as amended), a nominee is a _beneficiary_ in life insurance (not merely a collector for the estate, as in
other contexts), and claims with a valid beneficial nominee and clean KYC settle fast — the rest go into
paperwork measured in months.

**4. Excluded causes of death.** Suicide within the first year (Section 45A-style standard clause: within 12
months, only premiums paid are returned, and only 80% under some policy wordings), death during an excluded
activity (professional adventure sports, aviation for non-crew, war risk in some wordings), death from an
excluded pre-existing condition within the waiting period in certain riders. These are wordings issues: they
were readable at purchase.

**5. Lapse and reinstatement gaps.** Death during the grace period is covered; death after lapse is not. The
class of rejected claims includes policies that lapsed 3 months before death with the family unaware. Auto-debit
from an account that remains funded is the entire solution.

**6. Fraud — forged documents, fictitious deaths, misstated income at proposal.** Income misstatement matters
because sum assured is underwritten against declared income; an income claimed at ₹50 lakh a year to get a ₹2
crore cover, evidenced by nothing, is treated as fraud when claimed.

**7. Death in the policy's specific waiting or exclusion periods** (for example, accidental-death riders with
their own definitions, or the initial 90-180 day exclusion on some covers).

| Reason                          | Share of rejections (indicative) | Preventable at purchase?    |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Medical non-disclosure          | ~40-50%                          | Yes, entirely               |
| Nomination / documentation      | ~20%                             | Yes, entirely               |
| Excluded cause / waiting period | ~15%                             | Yes, by reading the wording |
| Lapse at death                  | ~10%                             | Yes, by auto-debit          |
| Fraud                           | ~5%                              | Yes, by not committing it   |

Indicative shares, compiled from insurer claim-declaration disclosures and IRDAI grievance data; the ranking is
stable across insurers even when the percentages vary.

## What the investigation actually looks at

For death claims within three years (and all high-value claims), the insurer appoints an investigator who: gets
the family's consent and pulls hospital and doctor records; checks the death certificate's cause; verifies
identity documents; checks the proposal form's answers against what is found; and interviews the family, the
employer, sometimes the doctor. The report compares _the deceased's actual medical life_ with the _declared_
one. The claimant's duty is not to make the investigation harder — it is to make sure the declared version is
the accurate one, which happens years earlier at the proposal stage.

## The file to prepare now, not at claim time

A claim settles at the speed of its documents. The standard list: death certificate from the municipal
authority, the policy document, claimant's ID and PAN, the nominee's bank account details with a cancelled
cheque, medical records if hospitalisation preceded death, police FIR and post-mortem report for accidental
deaths, and employer certificate if death was in service. What most families lack: a folder listing all
policies (a lost policy is recoverable, but slow), the nominee's KYC readiness, and a written note of where
things are. IRDAI's prescribed claim timelines are: acknowledgment within 15 days, and settlement or repudiation
with reasons within 30 days of receiving all documents; delays beyond that attract interest at bank rates plus
2% (a floor of 2% above the bank rate for life insurers' delayed settlements). The claims-protection
provisions are in the Insurance Act at
[irdai.gov.in](https://irdai.gov.in).

## The legality note

Disclosure at proposal is a legal duty under Section 45 of the Insurance Act: the insured must disclose material
facts, and the insurer's remedies are bounded — repudiation only within three years, only for fraud or material
misstatement, only with written grounds after inquiry, and with the premium refundable in specified cases.
Beyond that boundary the claims regime is protective: Section 45's provisos restrict repudiation for
non-disclosure of _survived_ conditions, IRDAI's protection-of-policyholders regulations fix claim timelines and
interest for delay, and the insurance ombudsman (under the Consumer Protection Act framework and the
Ombudsman scheme) resolves disputes without court. What carries consequences the other way: filing a claim with
forged documents or a fictitious death is criminal fraud, with prosecution under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita's
cheating and forgery provisions in addition to claim denial. For the honest claimant, the practical risk is
delay, not rejection — and delay has a statutory price the insurer pays, not you.

## The purchase-time checklist that prevents 80% of rejections

Disclose every hospitalisation in the last five years, every diagnosed condition, every regular medication,
smoking and alcohol honestly — pay the higher premium; it is the honest premium. Fill the nomination correctly
(Schedule format, full name, relationship, and consider the beneficial nominee provisions under Section 39);
update it on life events. Declare income accurately with proof, sized to the cover you need. Set auto-debit
premium payment with a buffer. Read the exclusions section once, aloud if needed. And keep the folder. Term
insurance is the simplest product in Indian finance — the complexity lives entirely in the disclosure form, and
that is where the claim is actually won.
