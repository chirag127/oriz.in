The first salary is the only month in which nearly every money habit is still unformed, and the habits formed
in it persist for a decade. The advice usually given is either moral (save more!) or product-shaped (open a
SIP!), and both miss the mechanics: a first earner's money problems are cash-flow-timing problems, product
selection problems and CIBIL-formation problems, in that order. This article is the system, with the numbers
filled in.

## The allocation that survives contact with reality

A ₹30,000 take-home (the honest median first-salary band across Indian metros after deductions) with typical
metro shared-housing costs leaves about ₹10,000 of discretionary room. The split that works is percentages of
the _room_, not the salary:

| Bucket          | Share of ₹10,000 room | What it is                             | Where it sits                  |
| --------------- | --------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Fixed joy       | 40% (₹4,000)          | Eating out, subscriptions, travel fund | Savings account, spends freely |
| Life quality    | 30% (₹3,000)          | Fitness, courses, the better mattress  | Accumulates as named goals     |
| Emergency fund  | 20% (₹2,000)          | Until 3 months of expenses, then stop  | Sweep-in FD / liquid fund      |
| First investing | 10% (₹1,000)          | Index SIP, started _after_ the fund    | MF folio, direct plan          |

The order is the point: emergency fund before SIP, because a first earner's crises (job gaps, medical,
family) are exactly the events that force breaking a SIP at a loss or borrowing at 36% from an app. The
emergency target for a first earner is three months of _expenses_ (not salary) — ₹45,000-60,000 for most —
reached in about two years at ₹2,000 a month, faster with every increment's 20% contribution.

## The first credit card: chosen as a CIBIL instrument, used as a debit card

At 21 with no credit history, the card to get is not the rewards-optimised one; it is the one that gets
approved and gets used lightly. The realistic options:

| Route                   | Requirement                    | Why it works                                     |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Secured card against FD | A ₹25,000 FD with the bank     | Guaranteed approval; builds CIBIL                |
| Entry-level unsecured   | First salary credit, 6+ months | Banks pre-approve against salary credits         |
| Add-on on parent's card | Parent's account               | Fast, but _your_ name reports thin               |
| UPI-linked RuPay card   | Existing account               | Payments convenience; not always bureau-reported |

The rules of use matter more than the card: utilisation under 30% of limit, full statement payment every
cycle (the "minimum due" habit is the debt spiral covered in the loans track), and no cash withdrawals on
credit cards (fee plus interest from day one, no grace). Two years of this history is worth thousands in
future home-loan pricing — the first card is a credit-file investment that happens to offer 1% cashback.

## The five first-earner mistakes, with their price tags

1. **Lifestyle locks signed before the fund exists.** The ₹18,000-per-month rental on the first salary, plus
   the bike EMI, consumes 65% of take-home before savings; a job gap then converts to debt. Price: the
   emergency fund never forms.
2. **The SIP before the fund.** Market dip in month 8, no cash buffer, SIP stopped, redemption at a loss,
   CIBIL untouched but confidence gone. Price: the investing habit dies at its start.
3. **The BNPL stack.** Three "pay in 30 days" lines across food, fashion and travel apps, none bureau-reported
   (until they are), each small. Price: the credit report learns about the debt only when one is missed.
4. **Insurance bought as investment.** The ULIP/endowment "for tax saving" that locks ₹50,000 a year for a
   decade at 5% effective. Price: 8-9% of compounding foregone, plus the term cover that was never bought.
5. **Tax-time avoidance.** Not filing when taxable (the employer's TDS makes it mandatory in most cases), or
   filing with the employer's Form 16 alone and missing the FD interest, resulting in notices. Price:
   234B/234C interest and the first notice of a lifetime, arriving in the second year of employment.

## The salary-increment algorithm

Each increment, before lifestyle absorbs it: 20% of the increment to the emergency fund until the target, then
20% to the SIP step-up, 60% to lifestyle guilt-free. A ₹30,000 first salary compounding 8% annually with this
rule reaches a ₹1 lakh monthly SIP potential in about 12-13 years without any financial heroics — the boring
arithmetic that actually produces the corpus, versus the fantasy of the one big bet.

| Year | Take-home (8% hikes) | Room after fixed costs | 20% to fund/SIP |
| ---- | -------------------- | ---------------------- | --------------- |
| 1    | ₹30,000              | ₹10,000                | ₹2,000          |
| 3    | ₹35,000              | ₹12,500                | ₹2,500          |
| 5    | ₹41,000              | ₹15,500                | ₹3,100          |
| 8    | ₹51,600              | ₹21,000                | ₹4,200          |
| 12   | ₹70,000              | ₹31,000                | ₹6,200          |

## The legality note

Nothing in a first earner's toolkit is grey, but three legal notes matter. Income below the taxable limit
still requires filing in specific cases (high TDS, foreign travel thresholds, deposits above thresholds) —
filing is free and builds the record. Nomination on every account and folio takes minutes at this age and
prevents the succession paperwork that ties up a family's money for years. And the "part-time job" that pays
in cash with no invoice is fully taxable income regardless of its form; the 44AD/44ADA presumptive routes make
reporting it simple, and the consequences of not reporting are the standard 270A penalties described in the
income track's freelance article. The
[incometax.gov.in](https://incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/) filing portal and
[rbi.org.in](https://www.rbi.org.in/) banking guides carry the details.

## The bottom line

Emergency fund first, index SIP second, credit card as a CIBIL-builder used like a debit card, increments
allocated by the 20% rule, and a monthly review that catches everything. The first salary's size does not
matter nearly as much as the system it installs — because the system, not the salary, is what compounds.
