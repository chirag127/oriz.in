Career breaks — the sabbatical, the upskilling year, the care-year, the restart after a gap — are now common
enough that their financial mechanics deserve engineering rather than improvisation. The break's financial
failure modes are predictable: the fund sized on optimism, the health-insurance gap, the EMI that was sized
for two salaries, and the re-entry that drifts six months past intention. This article is the plan, with the
numbers.

## Sizing the sabbatical fund

| Component                    | Rule of thumb                             | Example (₹60,000/mo single earner, 12-month break) |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Living expenses during break | Reduced budget, honestly computed         | ₹45,000/mo × 12 = ₹5.4 lakh                        |
| EMI continuation             | Sized for one income, not two             | ₹18,000/mo × 12 = ₹2.16 lakh                       |
| Insurance premiums           | Health + term, kept alive                 | ₹30,000/year                                       |
| Contingency inside the break | 15% of the total                          | ~₹1.15 lakh                                        |
| The "return costs"           | Courses, certifications, interview travel | ₹50,000-₹1,50,000                                  |
| **Total fund**               | —                                         | **₹9.5-11 lakh**                                   |

The honest test is whether the fund exists in _liquid_ instruments (sweep-in FDs, liquid funds, the savings
buffer) — not as equity that would have to be sold in a market dip to fund groceries. Building it: 18-24
months of aggressive saving before the break, with the break's start date as the deadline and the fund's
balance as the go/no-go gate. Starting the break at 70% funding is the standard way breaks extend past
intention.

## The five things to fix before the last day of work

1. **Health insurance that is not the employer's.** The group cover dies with the job; a personal family
   floater bought _before_ the break carries its waiting periods during the salaried months rather than
   during the break. The ₹10-25 lakh floater at 35 costs roughly ₹15,000-25,000 a year.
2. **The EMI audit.** Every recurring commitment re-tested against one-income solvency: the home loan, the
   car, the phone's device EMI, the BNPL lines. Prepay the small ones; restructure what can be restructured;
   know which are killable before the break, not during it.
3. **EPF and NPS decisions made explicitly.** EPF stays as-is (do not withdraw — the tax and compounding
   math almost never favour it); NPS contributions pause (it is a contribution-based product); the UAN's
   KYC is verified so the account is serviceable remotely.
4. **The credit line that is not needed until it is.** A personal credit line or an untouched card limit is
   the break's second buffer — arranged while employed (approval follows income), never used unless needed.
5. **The tax-file state.** Filing continues during the break (interest income, capital gains, the refund
   claims on any TDS) — an ITR on record is also the re-entry's proof-of-financial-life for lenders.

## What continues, what pauses, what dies

| Instrument       | During the break                    | Note                                                           |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| EPF              | Dormant, interest continues         | Transfer on re-entry, not withdrawal                           |
| NPS              | Paused                              | Minimum contribution lapses Tier-I status only if never funded |
| PPF              | Continue if possible (₹500 minimum) | The 15-year clock does not care about salaries                 |
| SIPs             | Reduce, do not stop                 | ₹500 folios preserve the habit and the average                 |
| Health insurance | Keep alive at all costs             | The waiting-period continuity is the asset                     |
| Term insurance   | Keep alive                          | Cheapest to restart is the one never stopped                   |
| Credit card      | Use small, pay full                 | Inactivity closure is a real risk                              |
| CIBIL score      | Monitored quarterly                 | The report read catches the drift early                        |

The PPF continuation deserves its note: a ₹500-a-year minimum keeps a 15-year account alive at trivial cost,
and the account's compounding is often the break-era's only accumulation. The SIP reduction rather than
stoppage is behavioural, not financial — the folio's continuity is what makes the restart a step-up rather
than a fresh start.

## The re-entry plan: protecting the income curve

The break's cost is not the fund spent; it is the income curve's reset. The counter-mechanics:

- **The gap narrative, built during the break, not after.** The project, course or portfolio produced during
  the break is the interview answer; a break defended retrospectively is a break apologised for.
- **The network cadence.** One professional contact touched every two weeks during the break — the re-entry's
  offers come disproportionately from the relationships kept warm, not the portals applied to cold.
- **The contractor/consulting bridge.** Part-time or project work during the later break months converts
  directly into re-entry references and keeps the ITR and the credit file alive.
- **The salary expectation, set honestly.** A two-year break typically re-enters at 10-25% below the exit
  salary; the plan that prices this is calmer than the one that discovers it in month four of searching.

## The legality note

Nothing in a planned break is grey, but three legal notes matter. Continuing ITR filing during the break is
not merely habit: the loan, visa and re-entry processes ask for returns, and gaps in the filing record have
their own friction. EPF withdrawal before five years of continuous service adds the withdrawal to taxable
income (with 10% TDS where PAN is on record) — the transfer route is almost always better, and the EPFO
[official portal](https://www.epfindia.gov.in/) carries the rules. And the employer's notice-period and
non-compete terms in the resignation letter are enforceable contracts, not suggestions — the exit that
respects them preserves references, which are the re-entry's most valuable currency.

## The bottom line

A career break is a funded project with a deadline: the sabbatical fund sized honestly in liquid instruments,
insurance and EMIs fixed before the last working day, EPF preserved and PPF's minimum continued, the re-entry
plan started during the break rather than after it, and a quarterly review that keeps intention and balance
sheet in the same conversation. The break is not the enemy of a financial plan; the unfunded break is.
