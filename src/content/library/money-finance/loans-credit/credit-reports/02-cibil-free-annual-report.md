Every credit bureau in India must give you one free full credit report every calendar year — it is written
into the Credit Information Companies Rules, 2006 (as amended in 2015), not a promotional offer. Equifax,
CRIF High Mark, Experian and CIBIL (TransUnion CIBIL) each provide it. The CIBIL report is the one lenders
pull most, which makes it the one worth reading carefully once a year, on your terms. This article is the
walkthrough: how to get it without paying, what each line means, and what to do when a line is wrong.

## Getting it, step by step

The route that works as of 2026: go to CIBIL's official site at
[cibil.com](https://www.cibil.com/get-free-cibil-report/), choose the free annual report option, complete the
KYC (PAN, name as on PAN, date of birth, address, mobile with OTP), and answer the account-verification
questions — the ones drawn from your actual loan history, such as which bank your home loan EMI runs with.
The report lands in your inbox within minutes to a few hours. Three cautions:

1. **You are entitled to one free full report per year per bureau.** Anything asking payment is either a
   paid tier (score monitoring subscriptions) or not the official channel.
2. **The free report includes your score.** The paid "unlimited refresh" plans are the upsell; once a year
   from each of four bureaus means four honest snapshots annually if you want them.
3. **Do it before any major credit application**, not after. Pull the report two to three months before a
   home-loan or car-loan application; errors take 15-30 days to fix and you want them fixed before the
   lender's underwriter sees them.

## Reading the report section by section

| Section                | What it contains                                                                          | What to check first                         |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| CIBIL Score            | 300-900, bureau-computed                                                                  | The number and the date it was computed     |
| Personal information   | Name, PAN, DOB, addresses, phone numbers                                                  | Addresses and phones you never used         |
| Contact information    | Reported by lenders                                                                       | Wrong mobile numbers (fraud signal)         |
| Employment information | Income and classification as reported                                                     | Stale employer data (harmless, but note it) |
| Account information    | Every credit facility: type, owner, balance, overdue, days past due, payment history grid | The heart of the report                     |
| Enquiry information    | Every hard pull of your report, with dates                                                | Enquiries you never authorised              |

The account section's payment-history grid is 36 months of D/P (days past due) markers, "000" meaning paid on
time. The fields that decide your next loan's pricing: **Days Past Due** (anything above 0 on a live account
is a red flag an underwriter will ask about), **Ownership** (individual versus joint — you are liable for
joint accounts' full dues, not half), **Settled or Written-off status** (a "settled" account reads worse than
a closed one; it means you paid less than agreed), and the **Date Closed** (closed-and-reported-late accounts
are a common clerical bug that keeps a dead loan alive on your report).

## The five errors that actually matter

1. **Accounts that are not yours** — name similarity, PAN typos at the lender's end, or identity misuse. A
   personal loan in your name in a city you have never visited is a fraud case, not a clerical one.
2. **Wrong overdue amounts** on genuinely closed loans, typically because the last EMI's bounce or the
   foreclosure charges were never reconciled at the lender.
3. **Closed loans still showing live**, or live limits showing as overdue — the "Date Closed" bug again.
4. **Enquiries you did not cause** — a broker or a card salesman who ran your report. Each hard enquiry
   dents the score slightly and too many in six months reads as credit-hungry.
5. **Wrong personal data** mixing two people's histories — the classic father-son PAN confusion where the
   father's credit lines land on the son's report and wreck his first home-loan application.

## The dispute process, with timelines

Disputes are filed online at CIBIL's dispute-resolution page (part of the same cibil.com site) with the
dispute ID of the specific entry, or by writing to the bureau. CIBIL's own committed timeline: the bureau
takes up to 30 days to route the dispute to the lender and update you; the lender then has to verify and
respond, and the practical total for a clean-cut error is 30-45 days. Two structural facts make disputes
faster: the bureau only reports what lenders send it, so the _real_ fix happens at the lender's end — escalate
there in parallel, in writing, with the loan account number; and keep the dispute ID, because the follow-up
query references it. If the lender does not respond, the RBI's Integrated Ombudsman scheme at
[rbi.org.in](https://cms.rbi.org.in) handles complaints against the lender after 30 days of no resolution.

## The hygiene loop that makes this worth the hour

Annual routine: pull the report, read the enquiry section first (freshest fraud signal), then account
statuses, then personal data. Fix errors in the same month. Calendar the next pull for the same date next
year, and rotate bureaus — CIBIL this year, CRIF High Mark next — because different lenders report to
different bureaus with different fidelity, and the bureau your main lender does not report to is the one
whose report you have never seen. One hour a year, four bureaus on rotation, and your credit file stays a
record you wrote rather than one somebody else is editing.

## The bottom line

The free annual CIBIL report is the only financial document where you can audit what the entire financial
system believes about you, at zero cost, in one sitting. The score is the least interesting line on it — the
enquiry list, the ownership flags and the closed-but-live accounts are where the money is. Errors there cost
EMI points of interest on the next loan, which is the difference between 8.65% and 9.4% on a ₹50 lakh home
loan: roughly ₹2.2 lakh over fifteen years. That is the highest hourly return in personal finance, and it
needs no investment at all.
