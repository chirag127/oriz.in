An annuity converts a corpus into a guaranteed income for life, and it is the only retail product in India that
does so with a promise the insurer cannot legally walk away from. That makes it the standard answer to the question
"what do I do with my retirement corpus on day one" — and, because the products are opaque and the sales channels
are commissions-driven, the answer people actually buy is frequently the wrong variant. Here is how the options
differ and what the money really earns.

## The product taxonomy

Every immediate annuity in India is a variant of the same bargain: you hand over a lump sum, the insurer pays you a
periodic amount until death, and the differences are all about what happens to the corpus when you die.

| Option                                     | What you get                 | What heirs get               | Typical rate on ₹50 lakh (60-year-old) |
| ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------- | ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Life annuity, single life                  | Income for your life only    | Nothing                      | ~7.2-7.5% p.a.                         |
| Life annuity with return of purchase price | Income for life              | Full purchase price back     | ~6.6-6.9% p.a.                         |
| Joint life, last survivor, 100%            | Income until second death    | Nothing                      | ~6.8-7.1% p.a.                         |
| Joint life with return of purchase price   | Income until second death    | Full purchase price          | ~6.2-6.5% p.a.                         |
| Annuity certain (15/20 years) then life    | Guaranteed period regardless | Balance of guaranteed period | ~7.0-7.3% p.a.                         |
| Inflation-linked (escalating 3-5%)         | Income rising annually       | Varies by plan               | ~5.5-6.0% starting rate                |

The rate spread between the purest and the most bequest-heavy option is around one full percentage point, and that
spread is the price of the return-of-purchase-price promise — roughly the cost of insuring your heirs against your
early death. A 60-year-old putting ₹50 lakh into a return-of-purchase-price plan earns about ₹3.3 lakh a year
(₹27,500 a month), and their heirs get ₹50 lakh back at death. The same money in a single-life plan earns about
₹3.65 lakh (₹30,400 a month) and the heirs get nothing. Which is better is an actuarial question about your
longevity, and a family question about whether the bequest matters.

## What the yield really is

The headline "annuity rate" is the payout as a percentage of purchase price in year one — it is not a return on
capital, because the capital is being returned to you inside the payments (or, under ROPP, to your heirs at the
end). The honest internal rate of return depends on how long you live:

| Age at death | IRR, single-life @7.3% on ₹50 lakh | IRR, ROPP @6.7% on ₹50 lakh |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| 70           | ~2.6%                              | ~4.5%                       |
| 75           | ~4.5%                              | ~5.8%                       |
| 80           | ~5.5%                              | ~6.4%                       |
| 85           | ~6.2%                              | ~6.7%                       |
| 90           | ~6.6%                              | ~6.8%                       |

Read this table the right way: the single-life annuity is a bet on your own longevity, and it pays the house —
here, the insurer's pool — if you die early. The ROPP option has a floor (your heirs get the corpus) and a lower
ceiling (your IRR asymptotes near the payout rate). For a household with dependants and no other bequest assets,
ROPP is usually the defensible default; for a single retiree whose estate matters to no one, taking the higher
single-life rate is rational.

## The LIC question

Life Insurance Corporation of India dominates this market the way it dominates nothing else in Indian finance, and
the reasons are partly real and partly historical. The real part: sovereign backing by perception (LIC is
statutorily backed under the LIC Act, and its policyholder liabilities carry an explicit government guarantee on
sums assured up to ₹5 lakh under Section 16 of the 1956 Act — a provision written for a different era and one that
has never been tested at scale), an unmatched claims-record reputation, and branch access in every district. The
historical part: distribution. LIC's Jeevan Akshay (immediate annuity) and Jeevan Shanti (deferred annuity) are the
products most agents quote first, and their rates are broadly competitive — often within 10-25 basis points of
private peers — but not consistently better.

| Insurer (illustrative)           | Single-life 60-year-old | ROPP 60-year-old |
| -------------------------------- | ----------------------- | ---------------- |
| LIC Jeevan Akshay-VII            | ~7.2-7.4%               | ~6.6-6.8%        |
| HDFC Life Sanchay Plus / annuity | ~7.3-7.5%               | ~6.5-6.7%        |
| SBI Life annuity                 | ~7.1-7.3%               | ~6.4-6.6%        |
| ICICI Pru immediate annuity      | ~7.3-7.5%               | ~6.5-6.7%        |
| Tata AIA / Bajaj variants        | ~7.2-7.5%               | ~6.4-6.7%        |

Rates move with the G-sec curve and are reset by each insurer's pricing committee; the ranges above are indicative
for 2025-26 quotes on standard ages and must be re-verified with fresh quotes at purchase — no published table
substitutes for the actual illustration document. The comparison site of record for policy provisions is
[licindia.in](https://licindia.in/), where Jeevan Akshay and Jeevan Shanti benefit tables are published; use it to
check option definitions, not to shop on rate. Private insurers' products are sold through their own sites and
agents, and the IRDAI's consumer education pages explain the mandatory illustration format.

The honest assessment of LIC: buy it if branch permanence and the claims reputation matter to you, or if you want
a single counterparty for a 25-year income stream and price the comfort at 10-20 basis points. Do not buy it on the
belief that private insurers are less safe — every Indian life insurer is regulated by IRDAI with identical
solvency discipline, and policyholder protection does not depend on the brand.

## Deferred annuity and the NPS connection

A deferred annuity (Jeevan Shanti type) buys income starting at a future date — you pay now, vesting happens at 45,
60 or a chosen year, and the payout rate is locked at purchase. The trap is that locking today's rate for a
decade-out purchase means accepting today's interest environment; if rates rise, you have pre-committed. The more
flexible structure for most people is: keep the corpus invested, and buy the annuity _at_ retirement, using the
then-current rates.

The one place annuitisation is already mandatory is NPS: at exit, 40 per cent of the corpus must buy an annuity
from an empanelled insurer (40 per cent is tax-exempt at exit precisely because it is annuitised). NPS subscribers
choose the insurer and the annuity option at exit, from the same menu described above — and the same ROPP-vs-
single-life arithmetic applies. The tax asymmetry matters here: an annuity bought with NPS corpus gets its
exemption by statute, whereas an annuity bought from a taxable corpus (say, a matured FD or a sold property) gets
no such exemption — the purchase price is your own after-tax money, but the payouts are fully taxed as income.

## The tax of it, and the verdict

Annuity income is taxed as income from other sources at slab rates, every year, with no indexation and no special
treatment. For a 30 per cent slab retiree, a 7 per cent annuity is a 4.9 per cent post-tax yield — which is why the
honest sequence for most retirees is: fund the year-one to year-three expense bucket from FDs or liquid funds,
put the 4-8 year bucket into debt funds or senior-citizen FDs, and buy the annuity with the portion of the corpus
that must be guaranteed against longevity, sized at 30-50 per cent of the total. The annuity is the longevity
insurance leg, not the whole portfolio.
