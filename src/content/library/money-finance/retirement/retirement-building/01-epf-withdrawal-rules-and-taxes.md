Most people meet the EPF withdrawal system exactly once in a panic — the week they resign — and discover that the
rules are nothing like the three-line summary their HR gave them. The Employees' Provident Fund is not one account
but two: a provident fund (your and your employer's savings, earning declared interest) and a pension fund (EPS)
whose money you do not "own" in the same way. Each has its own withdrawal path, its own tax treatment and its own
timing rules. This article is the map.

## What actually sits in the account

Of your 12 per cent contribution on basic wages plus DA, all of it goes to EPF. The employer's 12 per cent splits:
8.33 per cent funds the pension (EPS), but only on wages up to the ₹15,000 ceiling, so the maximum monthly EPS
contribution is ₹1,250; the balance goes to your EPF. Interest is declared annually by the Central Board of
Trustees and credited to the passbook:

| Financial year | EPF interest rate |
| -------------- | ----------------- |
| 2020-21        | 8.50%             |
| 2021-22        | 8.10%             |
| 2022-23        | 8.15%             |
| 2023-24        | 8.25%             |
| 2024-25        | 8.25%             |

The FY 2025-26 rate is set at the Board's meeting around February-March 2026; check the EPFO site before assuming
8.25 per cent continues. Note one quiet change from Finance Act 2021: interest on _your_ contributions above
₹2.5 lakh in a year (₹5 lakh in establishments where the employer contributes nothing) is taxable from FY 2021-22
onward. High earners shovelling bonuses into EPF should track this; the passbook now shows it.

## Full withdrawal: when and how

A final settlement (Form 19) is payable when you retire at 58, or when you remain unemployed for **more than two
consecutive months**. The unemployment clause is the exit everyone quietly uses after a resignation, and it is
legal — the two-month clock exists to stop same-week job hopping with the money, not to punish a real break. On
the claim, you also settle the pension side: if EPS service is under 10 years you can withdraw the entire pension
corpus (Form 10C); at 10 years or more, the sensible default is a scheme certificate that preserves the service
years for a pension at 58, though you may still choose to cash out.

The mechanics are online through the UAN: your Aadhaar must be verified, bank account and IFSC seeded and
validated, and the exit date recorded by the employer. Claims filed on a properly seeded account are mostly
auto-processed in days; the ones that stall are the ones where the employer's last contribution month does not
match the declared exit date. Check the passbook for a credited final month _before_ filing.

## Partial advances: the 2024 reform

Since 1 October 2024, EPFO has collapsed the earlier tangle of advance types into three ordinary categories plus
housing and unemployment:

| Advance type                               | Service needed   | Limit                                                          |
| ------------------------------------------ | ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Illness                                    | None             | Lower of 6 months' basic + DA, or employee share with interest |
| Education                                  | None             | 50% of employee share with interest                            |
| Marriage (self, children, siblings)        | None             | 50% of employee share with interest                            |
| Unemployment                               | 1 month jobless  | Up to 75% of corpus; balance on full settlement                |
| Housing (purchase/plot/repay housing loan) | Per scheme rules | Capped share of corpus, paid to the institution                |

The seven-year service conditions that used to gate marriage and education advances were dropped, and you may now
apply through Form 31 online. Two cautions survive the reform. First, an advance is a withdrawal of principal, not
a loan — there is no repayment, so the money stops compounding at the EPF rate permanently. Second, employers
processing advances against "medical" reasons do not verify hospital bills in most cases, but a withdrawal made
under a false ground is a misdeclaration against a statutory scheme, and repeat-use gaming is the kind of thing a
future audit round can catch.

## The tax rules, line by line

Withdrawal tax depends on exactly two things: your years of _continuous_ service (including service with an
employer the account was transferred from) and your income tax slab.

| Situation                                     | Tax on EPF interest and balance   | TDS                    |
| --------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| 5+ years continuous service at withdrawal     | Fully exempt under Section 10(12) | None                   |
| Under 5 years, total withdrawal below ₹50,000 | Taxable; add to income            | None                   |
| Under 5 years, ₹50,000 or more, PAN given     | Taxable                           | 10% under Section 192A |
| Under 5 years, ₹50,000 or more, no PAN        | Taxable                           | Maximum marginal rate  |
| Form 15G/15H filed, tax liability nil         | Taxable on paper, no TDS          | None                   |

"Taxable" unpacks like this: the employer's contribution and the interest on it are taxed as salary income in the
year of withdrawal; interest on your own contribution is taxed as other income; your own principal is not taxed
again because you were taxed on it when you earned it. Any 80C deduction you took for those employee contributions
gets added back. If you are under five years and in the 30 per cent bracket, a ₹6 lakh withdrawal can therefore
cost you well over ₹1.5 lakh in tax — which is why the standard fix is a **transfer, not a withdrawal**: moving the
balance to a new employer's EPF account (Form 13 / one-click transfer on the UAN portal) resets nothing but
preserves the service clock, because continuity across employers counts toward the five years.

One more recurring trap: the pension withdrawal under Form 10C is taxed on the same five-year logic, but the _EPS
monthly pension_ you draw after 58 under Form 10D is ordinary salary income, taxed at slab rates every year. The
₹7,500 ceiling on EPS pension (pensionable salary of ₹15,000 × 35 years ÷ 70) is the arithmetic that makes people
fight the higher-pension battle under the Supreme Court's November 2022 verdict; the opt-in window closed in 2023
and the follow-on litigation is still working through the courts.

## When withdrawing beats not withdrawing

The honest answer: rarely. At 8.25 per cent, tax-free, with zero maintenance effort, EPF is the best fixed-income
instrument most salaried people can access, and every rupee pulled out is replaced, if you are disciplined, by
something worse and, if you are not, by consumption. The defensible withdrawals are: the unemployment advance when
income has genuinely stopped; illness costs beyond what insurance covers; and the full settlement when a 58-year-old
needs the annuity ladder funded. The indefensible ones are the "I'll reinvest it" withdrawals — the honest record
of Indian behaviour is that a large share of withdrawn EPF money is spent within two years, and the tax department
at least takes its cut on the way.

## The paperwork checklist

Before any claim: verify Aadhaar against UAN, seed and validate the bank account, confirm the employer has marked
the exit date, and download the passbook as evidence of the last credited month. Forms that matter: 19 (final EPF
settlement), 10C (pension withdrawal or scheme certificate), 10D (monthly pension), 31 (partial advance), and the
composite claim form where Aadhaar is not seeded. The official FAQ and form pages live on the EPFO site at
[epfindia.gov.in](https://www.epfindia.gov.in/site_en/FAQ.php) — read them there rather than trusting aggregator
articles, because the advance rules changed in October 2024 and much of what is still published predates them.
