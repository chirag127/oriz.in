Zero-based budgeting has one rule: income minus assigned rupees equals zero, on paper, before the month begins. Not
"we will save whatever is left over" — that number is usually zero. Not "roughly 50/30/20" — that is a guideline
for people who have never met an Indian month with two weddings and a school-fee quarter. The zero-based version
assigns every rupee a job: ₹1 to groceries is a decision, and so is the ₹1 that goes to the SIP, and so is the ₹1
that quietly leaks to food delivery apps. When the plan is made, nothing is unassigned; when the month runs, the
plan tells you which decision to revise, instead of leaving you to wonder where the money went.

## The mechanics, in one page

Start from expected take-home for the month — not gross, not CTC, the number that lands. List every outflow
category, then assign amounts until the running total equals income exactly. If there is a surplus, it does not sit
unassigned: it goes into an emergency-fund or investment line by name. If there is a deficit, cut a named category
until it balances. The budget balances before the month, not after it.

| Line | Category                  | Assignment logic                                                              | Share of take-home (typical) |
| ---- | ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| 1    | Fixed commitments         | Rent, EMIs, insurance premiums, school fees — contract amounts, not estimates | 30–45%                       |
| 2    | Survival variable         | Groceries, commute, utilities, phone, domestic help                           | 15–20%                       |
| 3    | Lifestyle variable        | Eating out, entertainment, shopping, travel bookings                          | 10–15%                       |
| 4    | Irregular / sinking funds | Insurance annuals, festivals, repairs, gifts, subscriptions                   | 5–10%                        |
| 5    | Savings and investments   | Emergency fund top-up, SIPs, PPF, NPS                                         | 20–35%                       |

The last row is a line in the budget, not a residue. That single rearrangement — savings as a scheduled outflow
rather than a leftover — is the entire difference between zero-based budgeting and the ordinary kind.

## The categories Indian months actually contain

Standard templates fail here because they do not have lines for the expenses that make Indian months lumpy. A
workable zero-based budget carries these from month one:

| Expense                      | Why it needs its own line                          | Handling                              |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| School fees (quarterly)      | ₹30,000 in March is not a "March problem"          | ₹10,000/month sinking fund            |
| Insurance premiums (annual)  | Health, term, motor, two-wheeler renewals cluster  | 1/12th of the annual total each month |
| Festivals and travel         | Diwali, Dussehra, the December trip                | Estimate the year, divide by 12       |
| Family support / gifting     | Parents, siblings, weddings — real and recurring   | Fixed monthly assignment, not ad-hoc  |
| Home and vehicle maintenance | Servicing, repairs, society paint, appliance death | ₹2,000–4,000/month sinking fund       |
| Subscriptions                | OTT, cloud, gym, the app you forgot                | Quarterly audit; kill the unused      |

A ₹30,000 school-fee quarter paid from one month's cash flow either forces a credit-card rollover at 40%+ annual
rate or cannibalises the month's SIP. Pre-funded, it is a non-event. The category names matter less than the
existence of the lines; "miscellaneous" is banned because it is where budgets go to die.

## A worked example: ₹85,000 take-home

A single earner in a tier-1 city, one home-loan EMI, no car. Income ₹85,000; the assignment must total ₹85,000.

| Category                                         | Amount (₹) | Notes                         |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ---------- | ----------------------------- |
| Rent + maintenance                               | 22,000     | Contract amount               |
| Home-loan EMI                                    | 14,000     | Fixed                         |
| Groceries and household                          | 9,000      | Tracked, not guessed          |
| Utilities, phone, internet                       | 4,000      |                               |
| Commute and fuel                                 | 3,500      |                               |
| Eating out and entertainment                     | 6,000      | The guilt-free line           |
| Sinking funds (fees, insurance, travel, repairs) | 8,500      | Rises to 12,000 in fee months |
| Health and term insurance premiums               | 3,500      | Annual premiums ÷ 12          |
| Emergency-fund top-up                            | 4,500      | Until 6-month target met      |
| SIPs (index + flexi-cap)                         | 9,000      | Same-day as salary            |
| PPF contribution                                 | 1,500      | ₹1,500/month = ₹18,000/year   |

Total: ₹85,000. Zero unassigned. If the month overspends on eating out, the repair is explicit: move ₹2,000 from
the travel sinking fund, note it, and return it next month. What is banned is the silent overdraft of "adjust ho
jayega".

## Making it survive month two

**Write it down where you will see it.** A spreadsheet, a notes app, a notebook — the medium is irrelevant;
visibility is not. **Reconcile weekly, not monthly.** A 10-minute Sunday review of bank and UPI spend against the
plan catches drift while it is still ₹2,000 of drift, not ₹15,000. **Use the 24-hour rule for unplanned spends**
above a threshold you set (₹2,000 works): sleep on it, then buy if it still matters. **Give every adult in the
household a no-questions line** — a personal allowance neither partner audits. Budgets that ban all spontaneity
are broken by the first wedding invitation.

## Where the money should sit while it waits

Zero-based budgeting pairs with deliberate account placement. The sinking-fund amounts accumulate in a separate
savings account or a liquid fund — visible, but not in the salary account where the debit card lives. Salary
account holds month-one obligations only: EMI auto-debits, bills, groceries. Everything assigned to future months
moves out the day salary arrives. The mechanics of splitting the salary arrival hour are covered in the salary-day
article in this track; the budget decides the amounts, the account structure enforces them.

## The legality note

Budgeting itself touches nothing legal — but two adjacent habits do. First, cash withdrawals show in your bank
statements and large, structured cash splits can look like structuring to a curious bank's AML system; if you deal
in cash income, keep records and declare it. Income from any source is taxable under the Income-tax Act
([incometax.gov.in](https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/)) — a "budget" that quietly excludes freelance
income is not budgeting, it is under-reporting. Second, if the budget includes lending to or borrowing from
family, put it in writing even in the family WhatsApp group: amount, date, intent. It costs nothing, prevents the
most common family-money disputes, and — if ever large — keeps the transaction explainable as a loan rather than
unexplained credit in either party's books.

## What a working budget changes

The measurable outcomes after six months of a working zero-based budget: the month-end anxiety drops to zero
because there is nothing unaccounted; sinking-fund lines stop turning annual bills into crises; the savings rate
stops depending on willpower because the transfer is automated on salary day; and every discretionary spend
competes openly with every other one, because the money to fund it has to come from a named line. The budget does
not make you rich. It makes your finances _legible_ — and legible finances are the ones you can actually improve.
