Gold in India is bought three ways now — as a metal in a locker, as a unit in a demat account, and as a claim on
an app — and the three ways are priced very differently for the same underlying. The jewellery store's 22K bangle
at ₹74,000 per 10 grams plus making charges, the ETF unit at the day's NAV, and the app's "99.9% pure gold,
starting ₹10" are not the same trade. Here is the full cost stack, in the order you actually pay it.

## The three instruments, structurally

**Physical gold** — jewellery, coins, bars — is the metal itself, with GST at 3 per cent on the value of the
metal plus 5 per cent on making charges (a composite rate of 3 per cent applies where making charges are not
separately invoiced), purity risk if unassayed, and storage risk that you underwrite yourself.

**Gold ETFs** are exchange-traded units backed by physical gold held in vaults by the fund, with the METAL value
tracked by the NAV, an expense ratio of 0.3-0.8 per cent, and GST only on the expense ratio's embedded costs —
the 3 per cent GST on gold does not apply at purchase of the unit, because you are buying a security, not the
metal. Demat is required.

**Digital gold** — the apps selling fractional gold backed by SafeGold, MMTC-PAMP or Augmont — is a _claim_ on a
vault operator, not a security and not a direct metal holding. You buy at the app's quoted rate (spot plus a
spread), the vault operator stores and insures the metal, and you may sell back or take delivery above a
threshold. There is no SEBI or RBI regulation specific to digital gold: the product sits on the vault operator's
contractual promise and the app's customer service, which is the single most important structural fact about it.

## The all-in cost, ₹1 lakh of gold

| Cost / feature       | Physical (coins, 24K)                  | Gold ETF                     | Digital gold app                     |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Buy spread over spot | 2-8% (jewellery: 8-25% making charges) | ~0.2-0.5% (NAV-based)        | 2-6%                                 |
| GST at purchase      | 3% on metal (+5% on making charges)    | None                         | 3% on the metal value                |
| Recurring cost       | Locker ₹1,500-₹5,000/yr                | 0.3-0.8% p.a. expense        | 0 (some charge storage after 5 yrs)  |
| Sell spread          | 2-5% discount at local jeweller        | At market price, ~0.2%       | 2-6%                                 |
| Purity risk          | Real; assay costs                      | None (fund holds LBMA-grade) | Low; vault operator's brand          |
| Liquidity            | Days, local market                     | Same day on exchange         | App buy-back, T+2 payout             |
| Minimum ticket       | A gram-ish                             | 1 unit (~0.01-0.1g)          | ₹10                                  |
| Regulatory umbrella  | BIS hallmarking                        | SEBI, mutual fund rules      | Contract law; no dedicated regulator |

Work the ₹1 lakh through the table. Physical 24K coins at a 3 per cent buy spread plus GST: roughly ₹1,030 of
GST and spread, and the coin resells at a 3-5 per cent discount — a round trip of 6-8 per cent, ₹6,000-₹8,000.
Jewellery adds 8-25 per cent making charges that recover nothing at resale (you are paid for the metal), so a
₹1 lakh necklace round-trips at 12-30 per cent. The ETF: buy at NAV, pay the expense ratio inside the NAV, sell at
NAV — a round trip under 1 per cent. The digital app: a 2-6 per cent buy spread plus GST on the metal, and a
similar spread on sell-back — a round trip of 4-12 per cent depending on the platform, before delivery charges
if you take physical.

## The regulation gap, which is the deciding fact

Gold ETFs are SEBI-regulated mutual fund schemes with custodians, audited holdings and disclosure norms — the
same regime that protects your equity fund. Digital gold has no dedicated regulator. The vault operators are
reputable assayers (MMTC-PAMP, SafeGold's trusteeship structure), and the gold is insured and audited — but the
consumer's recourse on an app dispute is the app's grievance process, then consumer forums, then the contract with
the vault operator. The RBI and SEBI have both cautioned consumers about the unregulated status of digital gold
products in their advisories; the honest framing is that this is a well-run unregulated product, not a regulated
one, and the grey status has **consequences** when an app or an operator fails: the vault operator's gold is
yours by contract, but there is no investor-protection fund or statutory recourse behind it, and no **prosecut**
ion framework specific to a failed vault — you are an unsecured claimant on a commercial promise.

Physical gold, ironically, has the strongest regulatory touchpoint at the moment of purchase: the BIS hallmarking
mandate (6-carat purity levels marked, HUID hallmarks mandatory for jewellery sold in India since 2023) is a
statutory consumer protection — and the grey zone around _old_ or _un-hallmarked_ family gold remains the
country's default condition, with purity claims resting on the family jeweller's word.

## The tax, which is now uniform-ish

Since the July 2024 rationalisation, gold's capital gains rules align across the physical-and-fund forms:

| Holding                        | Period                                                 | Tax                                                         |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Physical gold                  | Under 24 months                                        | STCG at slab                                                |
| Physical gold                  | Over 24 months                                         | LTCG 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh annual exemption, no indexation |
| Gold ETF / fund                | Over 24 months (for listed/gold funds post-2024 rules) | 12.5% LTCG, no indexation                                   |
| Digital gold                   | Treated like physical/other precious metals            | Same 24-month/12.5% structure as physical                   |
| Sovereign Gold Bond (maturity) | 8 years                                                | Redemption exempt for individuals                           |

Two nuances matter in practice. First, the ₹1.25 lakh LTCG exemption is shared across asset classes in a financial
year, so a family with equity and gold gains plans redemptions to use the exemption efficiently. Second, digital
gold's tax character follows physical metal in most practitioners' reading — the 24-month long-term threshold and
the 12.5 per cent rate — which is a rare case where the unregulated product's tax is actually clearer than its
regulatory status. The Income Tax department's authoritative position on capital gains rates for specified
mutual funds and metals is published in the Finance Act texts at
[incometax.gov.in](https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/).

## Which one for which job

| Purpose                                         | Best fit                                                                                                        |
| ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Long-term portfolio allocation (5% gold sleeve) | Gold ETF (or fund of fund if no demat)                                                                          |
| SIP-style accumulation in small amounts         | Gold ETF via broker SIP; digital gold only if no demat                                                          |
| Wedding jewellery, held to be worn              | Physical, bought at low making-charge moments (Akshaya Tritiya pricing is marketing; off-season buying is real) |
| Gift, instant, ₹500-₹5,000                      | Digital gold (accepting the spread as a convenience fee)                                                        |
| Collateral for a loan                           | Physical or SGB; digital gold's pledgeability depends on the platform                                           |
| Bequest across generations                      | Physical or SGB; the locker and the nomination matter more than the form                                        |

The portfolio answer is the clearest: for the investor allocating 5-10 per cent of a portfolio to gold as a
diversifier, the ETF's sub-1 per cent round trip dominates everything else. Physical gold's costs are a price paid
for ornament and culture, and should be accounted as consumption-plus-store-of-value, not as an investment — the
honest ledger for a ₹1 lakh jewellery purchase records ₹25,000 of making charges as spending, not as wealth.
Digital gold wins on accessibility for a ₹500 start and loses everywhere else except convenience.

## The delivery trap on digital gold

One clause in every digital gold contract deserves a headline: taking physical delivery converts your cheap claim
into an expensive package. Delivery charges, minting charges on coins and GST apply at conversion — typically
₹200-₹500 plus 3 per cent GST, and some operators add making charges on medallions. An investor who buys ₹10,000
of digital gold and requests delivery of a 1-gram coin pays the conversion stack on a product whose selling point
was avoiding exactly that. The rule: digital gold is a paper claim to hold or sell; the moment delivery enters the
plan, price the alternative of simply buying the coin from a BIS-hallmarked jeweller at a known spread.
