Every investor asks the same question: where can a rupee earn more than 20% a year? The honest answer:
more than 30 Indian avenues _can_ deliver it — none reliably, and all share three traits: volatility,
illiquidity, or work. Here they all are in one table — lock-in, minimum ticket, and what separates the 20%
outcome from the 2% one. Read the columns a pitch deck never prints first.

The columns that matter most are the ones a pitch deck omits: lock-in, minimum and "do the work". A 24%
invoice-discounting yield on ₹1 lakh is ₹24,000 a year; a 24% smallcap drawdown on the same ticket is the
same number signed the other way. Row 32 is the only one that pays a guaranteed 30.8% on the contribution
the day you make it.

## The four families

**Market beta plus (1–12).** You hold a market that has compounded roughly 12% a year in INR; the extra
points come from picking, entry timing or manager selection, paid for with concentration risk or fees. AIF
and PMS minimums — ₹1 crore and ₹50 lakh — exclude most retail investors anyway, which is acceptable: SEBI's
own disclosures are honest about the dispersion of outcomes there.

**Credit (13–16).** Lending at 11–22% while the bank pays 7%. The gap is the price of default risk, not a
free lunch: an AA-rated NBFC FD at 9.5% against a large-bank FD at 7% is 2.5 points paid for bearing that
balance sheet. P2P platforms sit in the RBI's NBFC-P2P category; invoice platforms are not formally
regulated as exchanges, so platform risk is yours to underwrite. Read the RBI's
[NBFC-P2P master directions](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=11969) before
treating either as a deposit.

**Physical and alternative (17–26).** Property, land, gold, silver, collectibles. The return arrives with
income-free holding periods, thin resale and — for collectibles — genuine expertise. Fractional platforms
operating as SM REITs are SEBI-regulated; unregulated "fractional" pitch decks are not, and the difference
is enforceable rights. For land, verify the title chain through the state registration department — the
[Department of Land Resources](https://dolr.gov.in/) portal links every state's land-records system.

**Self (29–31).** Your business, skill and audience: no distributor, no track record, no manager — and
historically the highest ROI available to an individual, because the skill is the asset and cannot be
redeemed by someone else. Small professional income also gets Section 44ADA presumptive taxation.

## What 20% means after tax and inflation

| Pre-tax CAGR                     | Tax regime             | Post-tax | Real, after 7% inflation |
| -------------------------------- | ---------------------- | -------- | ------------------------ |
| 20% (equity, long-term)          | 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh | ~19%     | ~12%                     |
| 20% (interest: FD, P2P, invoice) | Slab, say 30%          | ~14%     | ~7%                      |
| 20% (crypto VDA)                 | 30% flat, no set-off   | ~14%     | ~7%                      |

The cruel lesson: the same headline is worth nearly 12% real in equity form and about 7% real in interest
form. Slab-taxed interest needs roughly 28% pre-tax to match equity at 20% pre-tax: asset location comes
before asset selection, and the credit family is sized by required return, not headline yield.

## Three honest rules

1. **Size it like a bet, not a savings account.** Anything promising 20%+ carries drawdown or default risk
   that can hand you −40% or −100%. The "assured 30%" pitches are the ones that end in enforcement
   orders — the [SEBI website](https://www.sebi.gov.in) publishes its actions weekly, and their patterns
   educate better than any pitch.

2. **Do the work or pay a manager to.** The last column divides the table. Rows marked "no" deliver 20% only
   in lucky cycles; "yes" rows pay the prepared and nothing to the passive. Decide which
   investor you are before allocating, not after.

3. **Re-run the comparison every two years.** The 2020 FD at 5% and the 2025 FD at 7.5% are different
   decisions. Refresh the numbers from the [RBI website](https://www.rbi.org.in), which publishes deposit
   rates, and let your own comparison — not a distributor's printout — decide where the surplus goes.

## The retail-reachable core

Most readers will not touch ₹1 crore AIFs or private M&A deals. The realistic 20%-chasing portfolio for a
salaried investor is blunter: direct equities or smallcap funds (50–60%), a sector fund or two entered on
weakness (10%), corporate bonds or invoice discounting (10–15%), REITs for yield (5–10%), and the tax row —
NPS ₹50,000 under 80CCD(1B) plus full 80C use — which adds 30.8% instantly on the contribution at the top
slab. That core, rebalanced twice a year, is the practical meaning of "compare all the avenues": not owning
all 32, but owning the ones whose risk you can underwrite, and knowing why the others are
excluded.

## The tax-location punchline

Crypto sits at the bottom of the post-tax table — 30% flat, no set-off — and still gets marketed harder than
anything else. Equity sits at the top with 12.5% long-term gains above ₹1.25 lakh a year. The same 20%
headline is worth ~12% real in equity and ~7% real in interest or VDA form, so where you hold an asset is as
decisive as which asset you pick. Before allocating, read the current sections on the
[Income Tax Department portal](https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal) — they change in every budget,
and this table is only as current as the law beneath it.
