Every mutual fund in India exists in two versions with the same portfolio, same fund manager, and same
securities: the direct plan, which you buy from the fund house yourself, and the regular plan, which pays a
commission to a distributor. The only structural difference is the expense ratio — and that difference, 0.5–1.2%
a year depending on category, compounds into a sum that surprises everyone who runs it for the first time.

## What the commission actually costs

| Category              | Typical regular-plan expense ratio | Typical direct-plan ratio | Gap   |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------- | ----- |
| Large-cap index fund  | 0.20%                              | 0.10%                     | 0.10% |
| Large-cap active fund | 1.6%                               | 0.9%                      | 0.7%  |
| Flexi-cap active      | 1.8%                               | 1.0%                      | 0.8%  |
| Mid/small-cap active  | 2.0%                               | 1.1%                      | 0.9%  |
| Debt/liquid fund      | 0.5%                               | 0.25%                     | 0.25% |

The gap is the distributor's trail commission, paid every year for as long as you hold — typically 0.3–1.0% for
equity funds. It is not a one-time fee, and it does not stop after year one. Run the difference on a SIP:

| Monthly SIP | Tenure   | Assume 12% gross | Regular at 11.2% (0.8% drag) | Direct at 12% | Difference |
| ----------- | -------- | ---------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------- | ---------- |
| ₹10,000     | 10 years | ₹23.2 lakh       | ₹21.7 lakh                   | ₹23.2 lakh    | ₹1.5 lakh  |
| ₹10,000     | 20 years | ₹1.0 crore       | ₹88 lakh                     | ₹1.0 crore    | ₹12 lakh   |
| ₹10,000     | 30 years | ₹3.5 crore       | ₹2.9 crore                   | ₹3.5 crore    | ₹60 lakh   |

The 30-year row is the one that changes behaviour: the commission on one SIP costs roughly a middle-segment car,
paid invisibly, over a career. Two SIPs double it. Note the honest caveat: the gap assumes the fund's gross
performance is identical, which it is by construction — same portfolio, the difference is only the fee. Direct
plan performance is a mechanical consequence of lower expenses, not a prediction.

## Where the direct route needs care

Direct plans shift work from the distributor to you. The distributor's legitimate functions — paperwork, the
annual review nudge, the nomination reminders, the someone-to-call — disappear. The replacement stack:

| Need                           | Direct-route solution                                                           |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Purchase platform              | Fund house websites/apps (free), or MF Utility, or RTA portals (CAMS, KFintech) |
| Portfolio view                 | Consolidated account statement (CAS) from CDSL/NSDL — free, monthly             |
| Tax statement                  | Capital-gains statements from the RTAs each April                               |
| Nominations, KYC, bank changes | Direct with the AMC or RTA                                                      |
| The annual review              | The monthly review ritual, extended                                             |

None of this is hard in 2026; it is merely unglamorous. The CAS — one statement covering every folio across all
AMCs — is arguably better than what any distributor offers, because it includes demat and shares too.

## Switching from regular to direct: the mechanics and the tax

A switch is a redemption of the regular plan and a purchase of the direct plan — a taxable event in the same
year, at the same tax rates as any sale:

| Fund type held             | Switch tax treatment                                   |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Equity fund, held > 1 year | LTCG above ₹1.25 lakh/year taxed at 12.5% (FY 2025-26) |
| Equity fund, held < 1 year | STCG at slab                                           |
| Debt fund, any holding     | Gains at slab (post-2023 rules)                        |

Three practical strategies: **switch the SIP forward, not the corpus** — stop the regular-plan SIP, start a
direct-plan SIP, and switch the existing corpus in tranches across financial years so each tranche's LTCG uses a
different year's ₹1.25 lakh exemption. **Switch in a low-income year** — a year with losses elsewhere or a career
break absorbs the gains at low marginal cost. **Or do nothing and switch only future money** — the existing
regular-plan corpus keeps compounding at the lower rate; imperfect, but zero tax friction now.

## When the regular plan is defensible

Not everyone should switch. The regular plan is a rational choice when: **the distributor provides real advice**
— asset allocation, rebalancing, the behavioural help that stops you from selling in a crash; that service has a
value which may exceed 0.8% for someone who would otherwise make expensive mistakes. **The alternative is not
direct — it is nothing.** The person who would never set up a direct SIP but does maintain a regular one through
their bank is net ahead. **Small folios with high admin burden** — the absolute rupee difference on a ₹2,000 SIP
is ₹30–40/month; the case strengthens as folios grow.

The indefensible middle: paying regular-plan commissions on index funds, where the plan difference (0.10% vs
0.20%) doubles the expense ratio of a product whose entire value proposition is low cost. Index funds belong in
direct plans by default.

## The regulation underneath

SEBI's framework governs both plans: AMCs must offer direct plans in every scheme
([sebi.gov.in](https://www.sebi.gov.in/) — the mutual fund regulations and circulars on total expense ratio),
disclose TER daily, and commission structures are trail-based with upfront commissions restricted. The
regulatory direction for a decade has been transparency: you can look up any scheme's regular-plan TER and infer
the trail. The consequence of ignoring it is not a penalty — it is the quiet 0.8% annual drag that no statement
highlights and no app badge flags.

## The decision, compressed

If you read articles like this one and maintain your own reviews, the direct plan is not a decision, it is a
correction — worth 0.5–1.2% a year, forever, with the switch-cost being one taxable event you can sequence. If
someone is genuinely advising you and you would act worse without them, pay the trail knowingly, and re-test that
judgement every year. The number to remember: at 12% gross, every 1% of annual fee removes roughly 25% of your
final corpus over 30 years. Fee drag is the only variable in investing you control with certainty; everything
else is weather.
