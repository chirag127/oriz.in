Capital gains tax in India is not one rate; it is a matrix of asset class, holding period, purchase date and
granularity of exemptions that changed twice in eighteen months. The Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024, effective
23 July 2024, collapsed most long-term rates to 12.5% without indexation and standardised holding periods. The
result is a rate card that is now simpler than it was — and a set of transitional traps that cost real money
because the old and new rules overlap. This is the full card for FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27).

## The rate card

| Asset                                                     | Holding period for long-term | Long-term rate                                                                              | Short-term rate | Notes                                                                                   |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Listed equity, equity mutual funds                        | Over 12 months               | 12.5% (Sec 112A) above ₹1.25 lakh                                                           | 20% (Sec 111A)  | STT-paid transactions only                                                              |
| Units of equity-oriented funds, business trusts           | Over 12 months               | 12.5% (112A) above ₹1.25 lakh                                                               | 20% (111A)      | Same thresholds                                                                         |
| Other listed securities (debt MF units held listed, etc.) | Over 12 months               | 12.5%                                                                                       | Per slab        | No 112A exemption                                                                       |
| Unlisted shares                                           | Over 24 months               | 12.5%                                                                                       | Per slab        | TDS under 194LBA/194S varies by seller                                                  |
| Immovable property (land, building)                       | Over 24 months               | 12.5% without indexation (or 20% with indexation for pre-23-July-2024 purchases, at option) | Per slab        | Buyer deducts TDS 1% under 194-IA (2% from 23 July 2024 if consideration over ₹50 lakh) |
| Gold, physical; gold ETFs; sovereign gold bonds (market)  | Over 24 months               | 12.5%                                                                                       | Per slab        | SGBs redeemed at maturity by the government: exempt                                     |
| Debt mutual fund units bought on/after 1 Apr 2023         | —                            | Always slab-rate (deemed short-term)                                                        | Slab            | No indexation, no LTCG benefit                                                          |
| Specified bonds, capital-indexed bonds                    | Over 12 months               | 12.5%                                                                                       | Slab            | Per 2024 change                                                                         |

Three structural points the card hides. First, **the ₹1.25 lakh exemption under Section 112A is annual, per
person, and does not carry forward** — it resets every financial year, which is why realising gains across two
financial years can legally save ₹7,812 a year at 12.5% on ₹1.25 lakh of gains. Second, the 12.5% rate applies
_without indexation_ for acquisitions after 23 July 2024; for land or buildings bought before that date by
resident individuals and HUFs, the taxpayer may choose between 12.5% without indexation and 20% with indexation
whichever is beneficial. Third, capital losses: short-term losses set off against both STCG and LTCG; long-term
losses set off only against LTCG; the unabsorbed balance carries forward eight years — but only if the return
is filed by the due date under Section 139(1).

## Worked examples

**Equity, the common case.** Bought ₹5,00,000 of listed shares, sold at ₹7,00,000 after 15 months. LTCG
₹2,00,000; exempt ₹1,25,000 under 112A; taxable ₹75,000 at 12.5% = ₹9,375. No cess complication (cess applies at
assessment: 4% on tax). If the same sale happened at 11 months, STCG ₹2,00,000 at 20% under 111A = ₹40,000. The
30-day difference in holding period cost ₹30,625 — which is why the sale date is a tax decision.

**Property, the pre-2024 purchase.** Flat bought in 2015 for ₹40 lakh, sold in FY 2025-26 for ₹90 lakh. Without
indexation: gain ₹50 lakh, tax at 12.5% = ₹6,25,000. With indexation (CII 2014-15 = 276, FY 2025-26 in force =
363; indexed cost = ₹40,00,000 × 363/276 = ₹52,60,870): gain ₹37,39,130, tax at 20% = ₹7,47,826. Here 12.5%
without indexation wins. For very long holdings and high-inflation periods the option can flip the other way —
which is why the choice is per-assessee and per-asset.

| Scenario                      | Sale price | Cost       | Basis used          | Taxable gain | Tax                 |
| ----------------------------- | ---------- | ---------- | ------------------- | ------------ | ------------------- |
| Listed equity, 15 months      | ₹7,00,000  | ₹5,00,000  | 112A, ₹1.25L exempt | ₹75,000      | ₹9,375              |
| Listed equity, 11 months      | ₹7,00,000  | ₹5,00,000  | 111A STCG 20%       | ₹2,00,000    | ₹40,000             |
| Property, no indexation       | ₹90,00,000 | ₹40,00,000 | 12.5% flat          | ₹50,00,000   | ₹6,25,000           |
| Property, indexed             | ₹90,00,000 | ₹52,60,870 | 20% indexed         | ₹37,39,130   | ₹7,47,826           |
| Debt MF bought after Apr 2023 | ₹6,00,000  | ₹5,00,000  | Slab rate           | ₹1,00,000    | ₹31,200 @31.2% slab |

## The exemption machinery: Sections 54, 54EC, 54F

For property, Section 54 exempts the gain if the sale proceeds (restricted to the gain) are reinvested in one
residential house in India, purchased one year before or two years after, or built within three years. The
exemption is now capped: **gains above ₹10 crore do not get the Section 54/54F exemption** (a 2024 change). The
capital-gains account scheme lets you park the money before the reinvestment deadline; unspent balances after
the window are taxed in the year the window closes.

Section 54EC: invest up to **₹50 lakh** in notified bonds (REC, PFC) within six months of the transfer; five-year
lock-in; interest is taxable. Section 54F applies to long-term gains from _any_ asset other than a residential
house when the net consideration is reinvested in a residential house. Only one house may be claimed under 54F
at a time, and owning more than one house on the date of transfer disqualifies the claim.

| Exemption    | Applies to                                                               | Cap                       | Investment window                                          |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Section 54   | LTCG on sale of residential house                                        | Gain, capped at ₹10 crore | 1 yr before / 2 yrs after (purchase), 3 yrs (construction) |
| Section 54F  | LTCG on any long-term asset                                              | Gain, capped at ₹10 crore | Same, into a residential house                             |
| Section 54EC | LTCG on land or building                                                 | ₹50 lakh per year per PAN | 6 months from transfer                                     |
| Section 54GB | LTCG on sale of residential property invested in eligible startup equity | Gain                      | 6 months from transfer                                     |

## The traps between the rules

**The 23 July 2024 boundary.** A property bought in June 2024 and sold in FY 2025-26 gets _no_ indexation
option (it was acquired after the date); the identical property bought in June 2023 does. Holding-period
arithmetic can change tax by lakhs purely on the purchase date.

**The loss-set-off deadline.** Losses carried forward require the return to be filed by the due date. A return
filed late in good faith still forfeits carried-forward losses permanently — this is probably the single most
expensive procedural error individual investors make.

**The buyback recharacterisation.** Since 1 October 2024, buyback consideration is treated as deemed dividend
under Section 2(22)(f) — taxed at slab as income from other sources — and the cost of the bought-back shares
becomes a capital loss available for set-off, but only if you report it. Most investors report neither and pay
tax twice on the same rupee.

**The STT condition.** Section 112A and 111A apply only where Securities Transaction Tax has been paid on both
legs. Off-market transfers of listed shares between relatives at below-market prices, then sold on exchange,
can trigger rate disputes — take care with gifts and family transfers of listed equity.

**Harmonised rates do not harmonise holding periods.** Gold ETFs and physical gold share a 12.5% long-term rate
but the ETF's holding period is 24 months while an equity-oriented gold fund of fund's is 12 — the same
exposure, three different tax outcomes depending on wrapper.

## The legality note

Tax on capital gains is statutory; the _avoidance_ opportunities are equally statutory and the _evasion_
consequences are real. Not reporting a taxable gain is concealment of income: penalty under Section 270A of up
to 200% of the tax on the underreported income, interest under Sections 234B/234C, and prosecution under
Section 276C where the evaded amount exceeds the thresholds. Splitting a property sale into multiple deeds to
dodge the ₹50 lakh TDS threshold can attract scrutiny of both the seller's gain and the buyer's TDS default
(consequences fall on the buyer under Section 201, which makes the buyer liable for the TDS plus interest).
Also note the income-tax department's SFT reporting: high-value transactions — property, shares, mutual funds,
bonds — are reported by registrars, depositories and sub-registrars, so undisclosed gains are now matched
against AIS data almost automatically. Rules and notifications are at
[incometax.gov.in](https://incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/).

## What to do with this

The rate card is short enough to hold in your head: 12.5% for long-term everything, slab rate for debt funds
bought after March 2023 and for short-term non-equity, 20% for short-term listed equity, one annual ₹1.25 lakh
exemption, indexation only as an option for pre-23-July-2024 property. Everything else is timing: which
financial year the gain lands in, whether the loss ledger is available, and whether the reinvestment window is
open. Most tax saved on capital gains is saved by calendar management, not by cleverness.
