An income-tax notice is a letter with a legal identity. The section number in the top-left corner tells you what
the department believes, what it can do next, and how long you have. Most notices are routine intimations
generated by automated comparison of your return against the department's data — and most are closed by a
correct, documented response within the deadline. What turns a small issue into a penalty case is not the notice;
it is silence, panic-driven replies, or responding to the wrong thing. This is the decoder and the playbook.

## First: is it real, and is it from the department?

Phishing on the fear of tax notices is a mature industry. A genuine notice arrives (a) on the e-filing portal
under _Pending Actions → e-Proceedings_ or _Worklist_, (b) on your registered email as a PDF from
`noreply@incometax.gov.in` with a document identification number, or (c) by post with the DIN. Verify every
notice independently: log into the portal yourself, not via a link in the email. Since 2019 every
communication must carry a **Document Identification Number (DIN)**; a notice without a DIN is not a valid
proceeding. Verify at
[incometax.gov.in](https://incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/).

## The notices, decoded

**Section 143(1) — intimation after processing.** Not an inquiry: arithmetic comparison of your return against
the department's data (AIS/TIS, 26AS). Outcomes: no demand, refund, or a demand with adjustments. Deadlines: the
intimation itself says a demand must be paid within 30 days; you can file a rectification under Section 154
within four years if you disagree. Most 143(1) demands are TDS credit mismatches — fixable, not fights.

**Section 139(9) — defective return.** Your return has an internal inconsistency: TDS claimed without matching
26AS entries, balance sheet missing for business income, a schedule missing. The notice gives 15 days (often
extendable) to file a corrected return. Ignore it and the return is treated as invalid — the filing never
happened, which has cascading consequences for loss carry-forward.

**Section 142(1) — inquiry before assessment.** "File your return" (if you have not) or "submit the documents
asked". This is a real inquiry with real consequences for silence: best-judgment assessment under Section 144
can follow.

**Section 143(2) — scrutiny.** The department has selected your return for detailed examination. Two tracks:
**faceless scrutiny** (assigned to a random team, no officer in your city) or limited scrutiny with a specific
scope. This is where a chartered accountant earns their fee. Deadlines are set in the notice and the
proceedings happen on the portal.

**Section 148 — income escaping assessment.** The department believes there is underreported income (often from
AIS data) and reopens assessment. Requires a notice with a recorded reason, and under the post-2021 regime, a
prior opportunity to respond to the proposed reopening. **Do not ignore it** — this is the notice with the
largest financial consequence.

**Section 245 — adjustment against refund.** The department proposes to set off your refund against an
outstanding demand from an earlier year. You get the notice; you have time to respond if the demand is wrong,
e.g., an old demand already paid or under appeal. Check the demand status on the portal before responding.

**Section 156 — demand notice.** The outcome notice after any assessment or rectification, stating the amount
payable and the 30-day window. Interest under 220(2) runs at 1% a month after the window.

## The first 30 days, in order

**Days 1-3: verify and do not pay anything.** Confirm the DIN on the portal. Download the notice PDF, the
relevant return, and your AIS/26AS. Identify the section and the specific paragraph that names the discrepancy —
notices are required to state the matter; if they do not, your response asks for it.

**Days 4-10: assemble the file.** For each allegation, the document that answers it: bank statements for
interest, broker P&L and contract notes for capital gains, Form 16 for TDS, rent receipts and the landlord's PAN
for HRA, donation receipts for 80G. Every document dated and traceable to a source. This is the file that ends
the proceeding.

**Days 10-20: draft the response.** Point-by-point, fact first: "The interest of ₹1,40,000 reported in AIS
includes ₹80,000 from FD 003 at Bank X, which was jointly held with my father; my share is ₹40,000 as per the
attached Form 15G and the bank's certificate." Numbers, dates, documents. No arguments about fairness, no
philosophy about taxation, no emotion.

**Days 20-30: submit on the portal, keep the receipt.** e-Proceedings generates an acknowledgment with a
transaction ID. If the notice requires payment, pay and attach the challan. If the response deadline cannot be
met, file an adjournment request on the portal _before_ the deadline, not after.

## The mistakes that escalate matters

- **Responding by email or phone call.** Only portal submissions create a legal record. Advice given by phone is
  not an answer to a notice.
- **Ignoring the notice because "the amount is small".** A 143(1) demand ignored accrues interest under 220(2)
  and can escalate to recovery, which includes bank attachment proceedings under Section 226 — a far worse
  outcome than the original demand.
- **Filing a rectification that contradicts your own earlier return without explanation.** Section 154
  rectifications are examined; a rectification that changes income materially without the supporting documents
  invites a 142(1) inquiry.
- **Hiding income discovered mid-proceeding.** Once a proceeding is open, voluntary disclosure through the
  updated-return route (Section 139(8A)) or in the response is treated very differently from income the
  department discovers first — the difference between interest and penalty, and between penalty and prosecution
  in eligible cases.

## The legality note

The notice itself is the beginning of due process, not a verdict. Your obligations in the proceeding are
specific: respond within the stated deadlines (the Act and the timeline rules in the code of procedure),
produce documents when asked, and pay or dispute the demand within 30 days under Section 156 — non-payment
beyond that window accrues interest under Section 220(2) and can trigger recovery and, for tax arrears above
₹10,000, certificate proceedings with attachment of bank accounts and property. On the other side, the
department's powers are also bounded: reopening under Section 148 has statutory time limits (three years, or
six years and beyond for escaped income above ₹50 lakh in specified cases), requires recorded reasons, and is
challengeable before the Joint Commissioner for objections. Penalties for concealment run up to 200% of tax
under Section 270A, and wilful evasion above thresholds can attract prosecution under Section 276C — which is
why the correct strategy in any scrutiny is documents first and disclosure early. The e-proceedings portal and
the current timelines are at
[incometax.gov.in](https://incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/).

## The long-term fix

Notices are generated by mismatch, and mismatch is generated by incomplete reporting. The taxpayer who reports
gross figures, reconciles AIS in February, claims only documented deductions, and files by the due date under
Section 139(1) receives, over a career, at most one or two notices — each closed with a PDF. The remedy is not
fear of the department. It is a filing discipline that gives the department's computers nothing to find.
