RERA — the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016, in force since May 2017 — is the most consequential
consumer protection in Indian real estate, and it is routinely misdescribed in both directions: as a guarantee
that nothing can go wrong (it is not), and as a toothless formality (it is not that either). The Act binds
builders on specific, enforceable points and is silent on others. Knowing the difference is worth more than any
possession-date clause a builder's sales team quotes.

## What the Act mandates, precisely

Every project above the threshold (500 square metres or eight units, with states empowered to vary) must be
registered with the state RERA authority before sale. Registration obliges the promoter to:

| Requirement                                                       | Section    | What it means in practice                                                              |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Project registration before advertising/selling                   | Sec. 3     | No pre-launch sales without registration; advertisement must carry the RERA number     |
| Disclose sanctioned plan, layout, and project details             | Sec. 4     | What is on the RERA site must match what is built                                      |
| 70% of buyer funds into a designated escrow account               | Sec. 4(2)  | Withdrawals proportionate to completed construction, certified by engineer/architect   |
| No changes to sanctioned plan without buyer consent               | Sec. 14    | Structural and "minor" changes defined; buyer's consent required for major alterations |
| Compensation for delay: interest at the stated rate to both sides | Sec. 18    | Buyer gets delay interest; builder liable on the same rate for his defaults            |
| Defect liability: 5 years on structural/workmanship defects       | Sec. 14(3) | Builder must rectify within 30 days of complaint                                       |
| Agent registration                                                | Sec. 9-10  | Brokers must be RERA-registered to deal in registered projects                         |
| Advance cap: 10% of cost before written agreement                 | Sec. 13    | The token-advance era's hard limit                                                     |

The escrow discipline in Section 4(2) is the Act's structural innovation: the developer cannot sweep your
instalments into a sister project or a land purchase — 70 per cent of the funds collected for a project must sit in
a designated account, and withdrawals are released against certified progress. This single provision did more for
the "where did my money go" problem than a decade of consumer forums had.

## What RERA buyers can actually do

| Problem                                        | Remedy under RERA                                                                                                               |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Possession delayed beyond the agreed date      | Interest for every month of delay at the rate set by the state authority; plus withdrawal with full refund + interest (Sec. 18) |
| Area delivered less than promised              | Refund of the excess with interest; the carpet-area definition is statutory (Sec. 2)                                            |
| Plan altered without consent                   | Complaint to the authority; compensation or restoration                                                                         |
| Structural defect within 5 years               | Rectification within 30 days of complaint, at the promoter's cost                                                               |
| False promises in the advertisement/brochure   | The advertised amenities are enforceable; the Act treats the brochure as part of the deal (Sec. 12)                             |
| Builder collecting beyond 10% before agreement | Complaint; the excess is recoverable                                                                                            |

Enforcement runs through the state authority (a complaint, a hearing, an order) with appeals to the Real Estate
Appellate Tribunal, and the Act's Section 40 gives orders the recovery teeth of arrears of land revenue. Interest
rates on delay are set by each state authority — the standard formula has converged around the SBI's highest
marginal cost lending rate plus 2 per cent — and both the buyer and the promoter are exposed to the same rate,
which is the Act's deliberate symmetry.

The official text, rules and each state authority's orders are accessible through the national RERA portal at
[rera.gov.in](https://rera.gov.in/), which links every state authority's registered-project database — the single
most useful pre-purchase step is to look the project up there and read the _actual_ disclosure: sanctioned plan,
approved layout, the promoter's litigation history as filed, and the quarterly progress updates.

## What RERA does not do

The honest list of gaps, which the marketing never mentions:

- **It does not guarantee possession on time.** It guarantees compensation if possession is late. The remedy is
  financial, not the flat on the date you planned your life around; buyers whose holding cost (rent + EMI
  simultaneously) exceeds the delay interest learn this arithmetic the hard way.
- **It does not cover projects below the threshold**, or plots in plotted developments that some state rules
  exclude, or resale transactions. Secondary sales between two individuals are outside RERA entirely.
- **It does not cover land in the layout sense** unless the state's rules bring plotted developments in — several
  states' rules initially excluded them, and coverage evolved through amendments and authority orders.
- **It does not vet title.** RERA registration requires a title certificate from an advocate — a document the
  promoter procures. RERA's machinery does not adjudicate a title defect; that remains a civil court matter.
- **It does not enforce quality beyond the 5-year defect window.** Finishing quality, material substitutions and
  spec-sheet downgrades end up in consumer forums, not the RERA authority, unless they map to the sanctioned plan.
- **It does not police the builder's solvency.** A promoter who stalls construction because the company is
  insolvent moves the problem to the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, where RERA orders become claims in the
  insolvency process — the 2022 Supreme Court decision in _Pioneer Urban Land v. Govindan Raghavan_ line of cases
  and the IBC's treatment of homebuyers as financial creditors (from the 2019 amendment) is the relevant regime,
  and it is slower than any buyer expects.

## The enforcement record, honestly

State authorities have varied in vigour. Maharashtra's MahaRERA has been the most professionalised — published
orders, a searchable complaint database, quarterly progress certifications with teeth. Several other authorities
began as extensions of the housing department and matured slowly; the Uttar Pradesh authority's docket has been
dominated by the NCR's stalled-project crisis, where the remedy has come as much from government intervention
packages and the IBC as from RERA itself. The Act's strongest practical effect is ex-ante: project disclosures
changed what builders can promise, and the escrow requirement changed construction-finance behaviour. Its weakest
is the buyer's remedy in a genuinely failed project, where compensation orders meet a promoter with no money.

| Scenario                                                | RERA's real utility                                                           |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Established developer, funded project, 8-month delay    | High: interest claim is clean and collectible                                 |
| Stalled project, promoter under insolvency              | Low-medium: RERA order becomes an IBC claim                                   |
| Minor spec deviation                                    | Low: consumer forum is the faster forum                                       |
| Title defect discovered post-registration               | Nil: civil court territory                                                    |
| Unregistered project (bought pre-RERA or sub-threshold) | Nil for that project; some relief via Sec. 3 complaints on unregistered sales |

## The buyer's RERA checklist

Before booking: verify the project's registration on the state authority's portal (number on every brochure and
advertisement); read the QPR (quarterly progress report) filings, not the sales brochure; check the promoter's
disclosed litigation; confirm the sanctioned plan matches the unit's plan sheet; verify the escrow account details
in the payment schedule. At booking: insist the agreement for sale is on the state authority's standard format
(where prescribed) or at least matches Section 13's requirements; nothing beyond 10 per cent before signing.
During construction: track the QPR updates and the escalation clauses. At possession: the completion certificate
/ occupancy certificate and the defect-liability acknowledgement.

The grey zone that still traps buyers is the **carpet area conversation**: RERA defines carpet area precisely and
requires its disclosure, yet sale agreements still quote super built-up ratios that vary 20-40 per cent across
projects. The statutory definition is at Section 2(k) and the loading is a commercial matter — the buyer's
protection is the disclosure, not the loading, and comparing projects on carpet-area-per-rupee is the only honest
metric.
