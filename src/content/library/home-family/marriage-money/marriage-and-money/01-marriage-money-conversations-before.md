The pre-marriage money conversation in India usually happens by accident — a rent negotiation, a wedding
budget fight, a disclosure about an education loan. Handled as "a talk", it goes badly for a structural
reason: a talk has emotions and no outputs. Handled as an _agenda_, it goes well: eight items, numbers on
paper, one hour, two signatures. Couples who exchange spreadsheets before exchanging rings start married life
with something rarer than romance — a working financial partnership. This article is that agenda.

## Item 1: Net worth, disclosed both ways

Each person writes down assets and liabilities at current values. Not rounded, not "about ₹10 lakh" — the
number. A ₹6,40,000 savings, ₹11,20,000 in MFs, ₹3,80,000 EPF, ₹2,00,000 credit-card-and-loan debt, a bike
worth ₹90,000: total ₹18,30,000, of which ₹2,00,000 is negative. The exchange matters more than the values:
it sets the norm that money in this marriage is discussed with numbers, not vibes. Dishonesty discovered
later — a hidden loan, an undisclosed family obligation — is the single most-cited money grievance in Indian
couples' counselling; the wedding's cost is the wrong place to learn this.

## Item 2: Debts, with interest rates attached

Every loan, listed with its rate and EMI:

| Debt                  | Outstanding | Rate  | EMI     | Priority            |
| --------------------- | ----------- | ----- | ------- | ------------------- |
| Education loan        | ₹4,80,000   | 9.5%  | ₹11,400 | Prepay after buffer |
| Personal loan         | ₹1,50,000   | 14.0% | ₹7,200  | Clear first         |
| Credit card revolving | ₹60,000     | 42%   | minimum | Clear immediately   |
| Two-wheeler loan      | ₹80,000     | 11.5% | ₹3,900  | Normal EMI          |

The couple's combined debt load then gets one decision: the order of attack. The card at 42% is the only
emergency in the table; a ₹60,000 revolving balance costs ₹2,100 a month in interest alone. In most Indian
marriages one partner arrives with more debt and earns more; the agenda's job is to make that a joint plan
rather than a private anxiety.

## Item 3: Family obligations, quantified

The Indian conversation everyone postpones: who supports whom, how much, for how long. Parents' medical
insurance status, monthly support commitments, a sibling's education in progress, the expected contribution
to a family construction or wedding. Each person writes their realistic numbers — ₹10,000/month to parents,
₹3,00,000 contingent per medical event, ₹1,50,000 for a sister's final semester. Unstated expectations are
where money fights live; the agreement itself can be renegotiated every year, but the baseline must exist
before the wedding, not surface in year three as a betrayal.

## Item 4: Runway and risk, both careers

Both incomes, both job stabilities, and the plan for the bad year: whose salary covers rent and EMIs if the
other's industry sheds jobs. The emergency fund gets sized against the _combined_ fixed obligations — EMIs,
rent, parents' support, insurance premiums — with the standard 6-12 month range. A couple with ₹1,40,000 of
monthly fixed commitments needs ₹8.4-16.8 lakh liquid, not the ₹3 lakh one person brought into the marriage.
This item also decides the risk appetite honestly: the freelance partner's variable income changes what the
household's SIP can be, and pretending otherwise just schedules the first big fight.

## Item 5: Accounts, cards and credit records

The operational map: which bank accounts exist, which credit cards, and both partners' credit reports —
pulled free, annually, from each bureau. A partner's 720 score with two late payments is useful information
for every future joint loan; the home loan's pricing will read both reports anyway. Existing cards get one
decision each (keep, close, add as add-on), and the two-three card discipline gets agreed before the first
joint big purchase.

## Item 6: The wedding itself, as the first joint financial decision

Indian weddings average well into seven figures for urban families, and the couple's own contribution is the
variable worth negotiating _first_, before family expectations harden. The working structure: a number the
two of them set, a source (savings, family gifts, both), and a rule that the wedding's budget does not
consume the emergency fund or the education loan prepayment. A couple that runs its first joint budget
successfully — the wedding — has rehearsed every later one.

## Item 7: The operating system, provisionally

A first draft, not a contract: what percentage of each salary flows to a shared household account, what stays
personal, who executes the SIPs, the monthly fifteen-minute money review. Common workable split for two
similar earners: 60-70% of each income to shared expenses and goals, the rest personal, no questions asked.
The provisional version matters because it is revisable — the point of item 7 is that there _is_ a version,
written down, dated.

## Item 8: The paperwork layer

Nominees on every existing account and policy (unmarried people name parents; after marriage this changes),
health-insurance additions or a family floater switch, employer insurance's spouse-add window, and the
one-page "where everything is" document each partner updates. Nothing here requires lawyers. All of it
requires an afternoon — ideally before, not six months after, the wedding, when updating a nominee requires
explaining why it wasn't done at the time.

## The legality note

Marriage in India creates real financial legalities worth stating plainly: maintenance and alimony rights
under the personal-law statutes and Section 144 BNS; the husband's liability for the wife's necessaries under
contract law precedent; stridhan's ownership staying with the woman (and the IT Act's treatment of gifts
between spouses); and the tax fact that gifts from spouse are exempt but their income clubs back to the
giver under Section 64. None of these are reasons for distrust; they are the reason the disclosure is a
_two-way_ street — consequences of non-disclosure at marriage surface in maintenance disputes and
annulment-fraud petitions, where concealment of material financial facts has been accepted grounds. The
statutes are public at [indiacode.nic.in](https://www.indiacode.nic.in).

## The bottom line

The pre-marriage money agenda is not romance's enemy; it is romance's insurance. Eight items, two hours, one
document, reviewed annually and rewritten as the incomes change. Couples report the same thing about it that
long-married couples report about money generally: the fights are rarely about numbers and almost always
about undisclosed expectations. The agenda's entire function is to make the expectations explicit while
renegotiating them is still easy — which is before the wedding, not after the first EMI bounces.
