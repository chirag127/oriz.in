Marriage is India's largest financial merger, conducted with less diligence than a ₹5 lakh business
partnership. The conversations that never happen — whose income funds what, what happens to each asset if
it ends, who owns the house's EMI contributions — are exactly the ones the law will later answer in its own
default language. This article is the architecture while things are good, and the law's answers for when
they are not.

## The account architecture that survives

| Layer                       | Purpose                                      | Ownership/operation                 |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Her own account             | Her income, her compounding, her credit file | Sole name, her operation            |
| His own account             | Same, mirrored                               | Sole name                           |
| Joint household account     | Fixed household flows                        | Either-or-survivor; both contribute |
| Joint goal folio (optional) | Shared goals: home, travel, emergency        | Joint holders, split recorded       |

The proportions are the couple's decision — proportional to income, equal, or needs-based — but the
_structure_ is not optional, because each layer answers a question the other layers cannot: the own accounts
preserve independent credit files and financial identity (the women-money track's own-account article covers
the infrastructure), while the joint account makes the household's fixed flows legible and auditable. The
arrangement that fails is the single joint account for everything, which blurs income attribution at tax
time and makes either party's financial life depend on the other's operational habits.

## The prenuptial question: what is and is not enforceable in India

There is no statutory framework for prenuptial agreements in India; a "prenup" is drafted as a contract and
its enforceability varies by personal law and court attitude:

| Instrument                                | Legal status                                            | Practical strength                   |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Prenuptial contract (Hindu law marriages) | Not marriage-dissolving; may be read as contract        | Weak-to-moderate; courts have varied |
| Prenup under Special Marriage Act         | Section 40 of the SMA allows agreements before marriage | Stronger footing for civil marriages |
| Postnuptial/family settlement             | Enforceable as contract/compromise                      | Moderate                             |
| Registered will                           | Fully enforceable succession instrument                 | Strong — the real instrument         |
| Gift deed for specific property           | Fully enforceable with registration                     | Strong                               |

The practical translation: in India, the reliable instruments are the will, the gift deed, the joint-holding
structure and the documented understanding — not the wedding-week contract modelled on foreign law. Couples
who want financial clarity should spend the effort on those four, which are enforceable, and on the
record-keeping (whose money bought what, with which account's trail) that decides most later disputes anyway.

## The big decisions to make while things are good

1. **The house.** Whose name(s), whose EMI, in what proportions — recorded in the agreement's text and the
   bank's record. A co-borrowed home loan with both names on the title is the default clean structure; the
   "bought in his name, paid from her account" pattern creates a contribution question that later needs
   evidence, not memory.
2. **The insurance.** Term covers on both incomes (each other's nominee), health floater with both names,
   and the nominations reviewed at every major life event. The "one earner, one policy" household leaves the
   homemaker's contribution unpriced — the maintenance law partially corrects it, the insurance prevents it.
3. **The investment attribution.** Whoever earns, owns; genuine gifts between spouses are lawful (the gift
   tax rules and the clubbing provisions in the tax track's articles govern the tax edges), and the paper
   trail is what makes the attribution provable.
4. **The household budget's governance.** The joint account's monthly flow, the annual review, and the
   decision rule for large spends — the couples who never define the rule default to the louder party's
   preferences, which is not a financial plan.
5. **The wills and nominations.** Each spouse's will, each account's nomination, the demat and EPFO records
   current. Death is the merger's other terminal event, and its paperwork is what the survivor lives through.

## When it ends: what the law actually provides

| Outcome                      | The law's framework                                                            |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Maintenance (wife)           | Section 125 CrPC / personal-law equivalents; needs-based, income-tested        |
| Alimony/stridhan return      | Personal-law provisions; courts weigh earning capacity, duration, conduct      |
| Children's custody & support | Welfare principle; support is both parents' obligation                         |
| Property division            | India has no community-property regime; title and contribution evidence govern |
| Dowry and stridhan claims    | The Dowry Prohibition Act and stridhan's absolute-ownership reading            |
| Shared household residence   | The DV Act's residence orders apply regardless of title                        |

The property-division line is the one that surprises: without a community-property law, the flat in the
husband's name alone is presumptively his asset, with the wife's contributions provable through bank trails
and EMI records — which is why the contribution documentation (point 1 above) is the single most valuable
paperwork a marriage generates. The stridhan framework and the DV Act's economic-relief provisions are the
wife's strongest statutory tools; the
[wcd.gov.in](https://wcd.gov.in/) ministry pages and
[indiacode.nic.in](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/) carry the current texts, and legal-aid services exist at
the district legal-services authorities for those who need them.

## The legality note

The legal frame here is dense and real: the personal-law maintenance provisions, the DV Act's monetary
reliefs, the Dowry Prohibition Act, the benami statute (a flat in the wife's name with the husband's
undisclosed funds and his benefit is a benami risk, not a gift), the gift and clubbing tax rules, and the
Succession Act's will framework. The boundaries: joint holding with genuine ownership shares is legal; name-
lending for unexplained wealth is benami; genuine gifts between spouses are lawful with documentation;
undocumented "arrangements" that exist to hide income are not. The
[incometax.gov.in](https://incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/) portal and the ministry pages cited above are the
official sources for the current texts.

## The bottom line

Marry the finances deliberately: three-layer account architecture, the four enforceable instruments (will,
gift deed, holding structure, documented contributions), the five big decisions made while things are good,
and the one-page conversation that makes them explicit. Indian marriage law's defaults are income-tested and
title-based; the couple's own documented choices are almost always better than the defaults — and they are
only available while things are good.
