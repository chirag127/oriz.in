The Telegram stock-tip group is the modern Indian pump-and-dump: 50,000 members, a pinned message with
yesterday's "calls", a leaderboard of past winners, and an upgrade path from free tips to ₹35,000 "premium"
membership. The mechanics are older than Telegram — penny-stock ramping has run since the printed-tip era —
but the scale and the fee ladder are new, and SEBI's enforcement actions in 2023–2025 show exactly how the
pattern ends.

## The lifecycle of a pump

A pump-and-dump is a coordinated accumulation followed by coordinated selling into retail demand that the
group's own recommendations create. The lifecycle has five stages, and the stage you join at decides whether
you are a beneficiary or the liquidity.

| Stage           | What happens                                                         | Who profits                      |
| --------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| 1. Accumulation | Operator builds a position in an illiquid small/micro cap over weeks | Operator only                    |
| 2. Seeding      | "Calls" to the free group; price moves on the group's own buying     | Early group members, slightly    |
| 3. Hype         | Screenshots of wins, influencer livestreams, "insider info" claims   | Operator, influencers            |
| 4. Distribution | Operator sells into the new buyers the group generates               | Operator                         |
| 5. Collapse     | Volume vanishes, price returns to or below the base                  | Everyone who bought at stage 3–4 |

The targeting is the tell. Pumped stocks are almost always micro-caps with thin floating stock, because a
group of even 2,000 buyers moving ₹10 lakh each can double the price of a ₹100-crore-market-cap company for a
day. Large caps cannot be pumped this way — no group's buying moves HDFC Bank — which is why every tip in
these groups is for a stock you have never heard of.

## The fee ladder: where the real money is

The pump's profit is in the stock, but the _business_ is the membership ladder. The structure is standard
across hundreds of groups:

| Tier                      | Price               | What is offered                               | The catch                                  |
| ------------------------- | ------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Free group                | ₹0                  | Daily "calls", 60–80% "hit rate" claims       | The funnel; calls are cherry-picked        |
| Paid tips (monthly)       | ₹1,000–5,000        | Same calls, "earlier", plus "position sizing" | No SEBI registration required to sell      |
| Premium/derivative "desk" | ₹25,000–75,000/year | F&O calls with leverage                       | Where most losses concentrate              |
| Courses and "mentorship"  | ₹35,000–2,00,000    | "Strategy" certification, lifetime access     | The course is the product, not the returns |
| Capital management        | Varies              | "We trade for you"                            | Often an unregistered PMS — illegal        |

Each rung is sold with the previous rung's screenshots. The 80% hit-rate claim is a survivorship artefact:
calls that went badly are deleted from the channel, and the leaderboard is curated by the same person selling
the upgrade. No group publishes a complete, timestamped, audited call log with entry, exit and holding period —
because the complete log is the refutation of the marketing.

## The SEBI enforcement pattern

SEBI's 2023–2025 orders against "finfluencer" and tip-selling operations establish the regulatory position:
giving investment advice for consideration requires registration as an investment adviser or research analyst
under the SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations, 2013 and the Research Analyst Regulations, 2014. The
orders have covered exactly this pattern — Telegram channels selling stock-specific advice, individuals
operating unregistered advisory businesses at scale, and coordinated pump-and-dump in micro-caps with
associated WhatsApp groups.

The [SEBI enforcement actions](https://www.sebi.gov.in/enforcement/orders.html) page lists the orders; the
common outcomes are disgorgement of advisory fees "wrongly earned", market bans of one to five years, and in
the pump-and-dump cases, impounding of the unlawful gains from the coordinated trading. The regulator's
surveillance tools — exchange data on trade clusters, WhatsApp/Telegram message recovery in SEBI investigations,
and IP correlation between group admins and trading accounts — make the coordinated trades traceable after
the fact, even though the groups themselves feel anonymous while they run.

## The exit math for the member

Assume a ₹2,000 stock recommended at stage 3, with the operator accumulated at ₹1,200. The member buys at
₹2,000 after the group's hype; the operator distributes into the demand until ₹2,400, and the price decays to
₹1,400 over six weeks as volume vanishes. The member's economics:

| Scenario                                           | Buy price | Sell price | Outcome on ₹1 lakh       |
| -------------------------------------------------- | --------- | ---------- | ------------------------ |
| Bought at hype, sold on group's "book profit" call | 2,000     | 2,300      | +₹15,000 minus brokerage |
| Bought at hype, held                               | 2,000     | 1,400      | −₹30,000                 |
| Bought late, on the "second entry" tip             | 2,300     | 1,400      | −₹39,000                 |
| Operator                                           | 1,200     | 2,400      | +₹100% on the position   |

The distribution of outcomes across a group's membership is the business model: a few early or lucky members
show +15% screenshots, the median member loses the spread, and the operator takes the concentrated profit.
The average member's loss funds the screenshots that recruit the next cohort.

## How to use tip groups without being the exit

The honest version of these groups exists — as _idea screens_, not advice. The discipline that makes them
safe:

1. **Never buy on the day of the call.** The call's first-day move is the group's own buying; enter only if
   the idea survives your own reading of the balance sheet, shareholding pattern and promoter pledge.
2. **Check the registration.** A paid adviser's SEBI registration number is verifiable on
   [sebi.gov.in](https://www.sebi.gov.in) — search the name before paying, not after the loss.
3. **Demand the complete log.** Ask for every call in the last twelve months with entry, exit, and result —
   not the highlights. Refusal is an answer.
4. **Size it as entertainment.** If the group's calls get 5% of your portfolio and are individually
   researched like any other stock idea, the downside is bounded. If they get 50%, you have outsourced your
   portfolio to a stranger with a Telegram channel.
5. **Treat F&O tip desks as casino advice.** SEBI's own study found that around 89% of individual F&O traders
   lose money in the aggregate; a tip desk selling leveraged index options to beginners is not addressing
   that statistic, it is monetising it.

## The legality note

Free stock opinions are legal. Charging for securities advice without SEBI registration is a regulatory
violation, and running coordinated pump-and-dump is market manipulation — a different and more serious
category. Consequences for the operators are documented in SEBI's orders: disgorgement of advisory and
subscription fees with interest, debarment from the securities market for years, impounding of trading gains
in manipulation cases, and in manipulation cases with deceitful intent, prosecution under Section 24 of the
SEBI Act, which carries imprisonment. The grey zone for _members_ is acting on tips without understanding
that the person recommending has a position — there is no disclosure obligation in an unregistered Telegram
group, which is exactly why the disclosure matters everywhere else. The member's own prosecution risk is nil;
the financial risk is the whole game.

## The bottom line

A Telegram tip group is a distribution business with a stock attached, and the fee ladder is the product.
The pump's arithmetic guarantees that the operator's position and the median member's outcome are opposites.
Use the groups as idea screens with your own research behind every buy, verify any paid adviser's registration
before paying, and treat the 80% hit-rate claim — and every screenshot that supports it — as marketing until
a complete timestamped log says otherwise. The market is not rigged against you; the group is structured
around you.
