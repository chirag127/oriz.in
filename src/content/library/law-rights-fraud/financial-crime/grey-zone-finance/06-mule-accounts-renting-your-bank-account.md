The offer arrives as a WhatsApp message or a campus poster: "Earn ₹8,000 weekly, work from home, just lend
your bank account for transactions." The account-rental economy — mule accounts — is now the plumbing of
Indian cyber fraud: every ₹50 lakh of scam proceeds needs a chain of accounts to pass through, and the
channels that supply them pay the account owner ₹1,000-5,000 per transaction cycle. This article decodes the
economics from the renter's side, because the renter is the person the machinery is designed to break first.

## What the offer actually is

A mule account is your account (or a new one you open on someone's instructions) used to receive money that
is not yours and pass it on, keeping a cut. The layering logic: a fraudster in one city cannot receive 200
victim payments into one account — the account freezes within days under the cyber-crime reporting mechanism,
and one freeze is a trail. Twenty accounts each receiving ₹50,000-2 lakh and forwarding the money within
minutes gives each victim's money five or six hops before pooling. Each hop is a real person with a real KYC.

| Layer          | Who                        | What happens                                | Typical cut        |
| -------------- | -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------ |
| Recruitment    | Agent, Telegram handler    | Signs up account holders, collects KYC      | ₹500-2,000/account |
| Account holder | You                        | Receives and forwards funds, shares OTPs    | ₹1,000-5,000/cycle |
| Aggregator     | Converts to crypto or cash | Pools from many mules, moves value offshore | 5-15% of volume    |
| Organiser      | Runs the fraud             | Pays the above out of proceeds              | The remainder      |

The advertised earnings are real for the first weeks — ₹8,000-₹20,000/month happens. What the advertisement
never itemises is the liability attached to the same rupees.

## What actually happens to the person who rented the account

**The freeze.** A defrauded person in Pune files on the national cyber-crime portal —
[cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in) — and the complaint's money trail reaches your account as
_held_ funds. Your bank freezes the disputed amount (sometimes the whole balance, sometimes with a lien).
Since 2024, RBI and I4C's standardized freeze process has made these freezes faster and more widespread:
your ₹30,000 balance with ₹8,000 of "mule" money passing through it can freeze the entire ₹30,000.

**The police visit.** The trail's account holders receive notices under Section 94 of the BNSS (earlier CrPC
41A) to join investigation. Thousands of people per large fraud receive these notices — the quantity matters
because a cohort that ignored the notice becomes, in the case file, a conspiracy.

**The charge.** Here is the part the recruitment pitch hides: receiving stolen or fraud-obtained property
into your account, knowing or having reason to believe it is such, is an offence. Section 318(4) of the
Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (cheating) and Section 61(2) (dishonest receiving of stolen property) are the
working charges; the investigation's question is not whether you committed the fraud but whether you were
wilfully blind — and the evidence of wilful blindness is exactly what mule activity generates: transactions
wildly inconsistent with your income, forwarding money within minutes, no invoices, no purpose, SMS trails
saying "forward fast, keep 2%".

**The banking consequences.** Banks run internal fraud-risk analytics; an account with mule-pattern flows
gets debit-credit restricted, then closed under the bank's risk policy. Multiple closures across banks create
a profile that follows you — customers have reported being refused account openings years later, because the
closure reasons persist in internal systems.

## The legitimate-looking variants, and why they are still traps

1. **"Payment gateway for my shop"** — you receive customer payments for a business you can see and verify,
   and forward them. If the business is real and invoiced, this is a partnership; if the "shop" is a name
   and a UPI QR code, you are the mule with a better story.
2. **"Crypto P2P arbitrage"** — you receive rupees from strangers buying USDT through you and forward to the
   platform. This one has a legal middle: some P2P activity is genuine trading, but the same channel is the
   dominant conversion route for fraud proceeds, and the accounts that receive scam money freeze exactly the
   same way. The distinguishing test: does any counterparty's money have a provenance you could explain on a
   notice?
3. **"Loan-disbursement facilitator"** — you hold and forward "processing funds". This is the older loan-app
   fraud's mule variant; the 2020-2023 loan-app enforcement wave caught the facilitators along with the
   organisers.

## The economics, honestly stated

Renting one account for three months at ₹5,000/month grosses ₹15,000. A single fraud-trail freeze with a
Section 94 notice costs: the frozen balance for the investigation's duration (months), a lawyer for the
statement and any anticipatory-bail precaution (₹15,000-50,000 in real cases), travel to the investigating
station repeatedly, and the bank relationship. The expected value is negative before any criminal outcome is
counted, and the criminal outcome is not rare — the 2024-2025 enforcement waves against cyber-fraud
networks in UP, Bihar, Rajasthan and Bengal arrested account holders alongside handlers, with the account
holders' defence resting entirely on how much of the arrangement they can document.

## The legality note

Renting your account for someone else's unexplained funds is **illegal** where the funds are proceeds of
offence, and the consequences scale with the fraud: Section 318(4) BNS cheating carries imprisonment up to
seven years where the value exceeds ₹50 lakh under the value-tiered sentencing the BNS introduced; receiving
stolen property under Section 61(2) carries up to three years; and the PMLA can attach accounts where
proceeds of crime are traced, converting the freeze into an attachment. The defences that actually work are
documentation of your own: the recruitment chat, the agent's name and number, the instruction messages —
preserved, not deleted. Consequences for the organisers run to organised-crime statutes in several states.

## The bottom line

The mule offer pays ₹1,000-5,000 to rent the one financial identity you cannot replace and cannot afford to
have flagged. Every layer above you in the table earns multiples of your cut precisely because they have
transferred the detection risk down to the KYC layer — you are not a partner in the operation, you are its
firewall. The honest versions of the same money exist: a real part-time job, a real freelance gig, even a
genuine small business's payment handling with invoices. They pay less per week and cost nothing when the
police call the others.
